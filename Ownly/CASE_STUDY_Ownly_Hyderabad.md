# Ownly: Bengaluru to Hyderabad

**Problem Statement**

Ownly is Rapido's food delivery app. It charges restaurants no commission and takes no platform,
packaging or surge fee from customers, so the bill is smaller. In Bengaluru this worked fast — from a
three-area pilot in Aug 2025 to a citywide launch in Mar 2026, and by Aug 2026 it was doing **50,000+
orders a day, about 10% of the whole Bengaluru market**.

Ownly is now copying that same playbook into Hyderabad.

The catch is **why** Bengaluru worked. In July 2026, **1,000+ Bengaluru restaurants threatened to quit
Swiggy and Zomato** over commissions and arbitrary deductions, with up to 21,000 Karnataka restaurants
expected to join. Ownly signed an agreement with the restaurant association in that same window.
Restaurants were already looking for a way out — **Ownly walked through a door someone else had opened.**

**Hyderabad has no such revolt.** And our own research there found something Ownly's price cut cannot
fix: when we offered people ₹30 off, **92.5% would wait 15 minutes longer and 72.5% would accept a
late order — but only 35% would give up their usual restaurants.** Ownly sells almost exactly the same
restaurants as Swiggy and Zomato (**89% overlap**), about **17 minutes slower**, to a city where
**82.5% already pay for a Swiggy One or Zomato Gold membership** that cancels out the saving.

**The real problem is not the price. It is that Ownly is bringing a supply-side win into a city where
the fight is on the demand side.**

# Bengaluru: the market and Ownly's strategy

**What the Bengaluru market was doing**

Restaurants were angry. Their association put commissions at **8% to 28%**, and their complaints were
not really about the percentage — they were about money being deducted automatically after a customer
complaint, having to pay for discounts they never agreed to, and no itemised monthly statement. In
July 2026 they set a deadline of 15 August to delist from Swiggy and Zomato.

Customers, meanwhile, were fairly loyal: in our own Bengaluru sample **81% already paid for a
membership**, and they said they'd need to save about **₹50 every time** before switching apps.

**What Ownly did**

1. **Went in slowly, then all at once.** Pilot in 3 areas (Aug 2025) → citywide (Mar 2026).
2. **Took the restaurants' side.** Zero commission, no subscription, no ad fees — the exact opposite
   of every complaint restaurants had. Signed an agreement with the national restaurant association.
3. **Made the customer's bill obviously smaller.** One flat ₹30 delivery fee, nothing else added on.
   Prices came out roughly **15% lower** than Swiggy and Zomato.
4. **Used Rapido's existing app** as a free front door (from Jul 2026).
5. **Accepted losing money.** Rapido's own executive: *"In the beginning, we're not going to be
   earning money."*

**Result:** ~20,000 restaurants signed up. 40,000 daily orders by July 2026, 50,000+ by August.

# Hyderabad: market and user behaviour

We surveyed **40 people in the Gachibowli area**, priced the **same food baskets on all three apps at
the same address and time**, and read **836 public posts, comments and reviews**.

**1. People will trade almost anything for ₹30 — except their restaurants.**
We offered the same ₹30 saving three times and changed only what you give up:

| Give up… | Will accept ₹30 to do it |
|---|---|
| Waiting 15 minutes longer | **92.5%** |
| A late order 3 times in 10 instead of 1 | **72.5%** |
| **Their usual restaurants** | **only 35%** |

This is not a small-sample wobble. 24 people accepted a delay but refused to lose their restaurants;
exactly **1** did the opposite.

**2. The saving is real, but most people never see it.**
Ownly was cheapest in **4 of 4** baskets, saving **₹114 on average**, because it adds almost no fees
(**4.8%** of the bill vs **23%** on the others). But **82.5% hold a paid membership**, and after one
ordinary Swiggy/Zomato coupon, **Ownly actually becomes ₹28 more expensive.**

**3. Barely anyone knows it exists.** 42.5% had heard of Ownly, 17.5% had opened it, **5% had ordered.**

**4. Rapido is not bringing people in.** 60% use Rapido, but only **5 of 33** had ever noticed food
inside it — and Rapido users were actually **less** aware of Ownly than non-users (33% vs 56%).

**5. Nobody believes the prices will last.** **87.5%** expect them to rise, and only **25%** would
keep using Ownly once a ₹100 intro offer ended.

# What reviews, social posts and videos add

**App store reviews (37):** **26 of them are 1-star.** The complaints are not about price at all —
**72%** mention a delivery that failed and **79%** mention support or refunds going wrong.

**Social posts (524, mostly Reddit):** people who have *actually ordered* talk about failed orders
**49.5%** of the time. People just discussing Ownly mention failure only **18.9%** of the time. The
gap is the warning: the closer you get to a real order, the worse it sounds.

**YouTube (275 comments):** the single biggest topic isn't price or restaurants — it's **rider pay**
(28% of comments, 1,167 likes), arguing riders earn more from bike taxi than from food delivery.

**One first-hand user summed up our own audit better than we did:**
*"Ownly is not that cheap… they have no discount on any restaurant… same menu available in Swiggy
with lesser cost after discount."*

**Interviews** told us why this matters: people don't leave an app after one bad order. They leave
after **repeated** bad orders. So poor delivery doesn't stop someone trying Ownly — it stops them
coming back.

**What the competition says.** Zomato's founder, publicly: *"…the same restaurants, similar or longer
delivery times… There's no new use case being unlocked here. The customer traction is purely
price-driven, and price-driven traction without structural economics tends to resolve itself."*
Our Gachibowli audit independently found exactly that — same restaurants, 17 minutes slower.

# What to carry over from Bengaluru

**KEEP — these worked and still make sense**

1. **Zero commission for restaurants.** This is the only thing Ownly has that Swiggy and Zomato
   cannot copy without giving up their own revenue.
2. **No platform, packaging or surge fees.** Fees are 4.8% of an Ownly bill against 23% on the
   others. This is a *structural* saving — it doesn't need funding, so it can't run out.

**ADAPT — right idea, wrong shape for Hyderabad**

3. **Stop saying "cheaper than Swiggy and Zomato."** For the 82.5% with a membership that claim is
   false at their own checkout, and any coupon proves it wrong publicly. Talk about the everyday
   bill instead of the comparison.
4. **Swap wide coverage for deep local coverage.** In Bengaluru, restaurants came to Ownly because
   they were angry with the incumbents. In Hyderabad that anger isn't there, so Ownly has to go and
   get the specific local places people actually order from — the one thing they won't give up.
5. **Use Rapido as a real channel, not a doorway.** Sitting inside the app isn't working: only 5 of
   33 Rapido users had noticed food in it. Interviews say people try new apps because a friend or a
   reel told them to.

**DEPRIORITISE — these are not earning their place**

6. **Big first-order discounts.** Only 25% would stay after the offer ends, and 87.5% already expect
   prices to rise. This is exactly how Foodpanda went from 200,000 orders a day to 5,000.
7. **Spending to match delivery speed.** 92.5% will happily wait 15 minutes longer to save ₹30.
   Speed is not what's blocking Ownly in Hyderabad.

# Solutions (in simple terms)

1. **Market Research** — Before copying Bengaluru into a new city, check whether the *reason*
   Bengaluru worked exists there too. Bengaluru's restaurants were in open revolt against Swiggy and
   Zomato; Hyderabad's are not. Ask people in that city what they'd actually give up for a cheaper
   bill — in Hyderabad the answer was "anything except my restaurants."

2. **Customer Base** — Don't chase the loyal member who already pays for Swiggy One. Go for people
   with no membership and people who already use two apps (57.5% in our sample) — they're used to
   switching, so becoming their second app is a much lower bar than becoming their first.

3. **Cost Control** — Ownly earns about ₹30 an order while the industry pays a rider about ₹56, so
   every order currently loses money. Until that gap closes, growing faster just loses more.
   Launch area by area, not city-wide, and prove the numbers in one area first.

4. **Increase Revenue** — Get the local restaurants people already order from (that's what they refuse
   to give up), so Ownly stops being "the same food, slower." Keep the no-fees promise, since that's
   the part that survives without spending money, and let people see the saving on their everyday
   bill instead of a comparison a coupon can disprove.

5. **Damage Control – Before it happens** — Watch two numbers every month: do people order a *second*
   time after the offer ends, and how many orders go wrong. Reviews say failed orders and bad refunds
   are the top complaints, and interviews say people quit after *repeated* failures. Fix delivery
   before spending on getting new users.

6. **Damage Control – After it happens** — If second orders don't come, stop the discounts rather
   than doubling them, and put the money into fixing delivery and adding local restaurants instead.
   Keep operating in the areas where the numbers work, and pause the rest.

# Full Solution

**Ownly's Bengaluru win was mostly a restaurant win, not a customer win.** Restaurants were furious
with Swiggy and Zomato and needed somewhere to go; Ownly offered zero commission at exactly the right
moment and got 20,000 of them. That door isn't open in Hyderabad.

So in Hyderabad, Ownly should **keep the two things that genuinely belong to it** — zero commission
for restaurants, and a bill with no hidden fees — and **stop leading with the price comparison**,
because 82.5% of the city holds a membership that erases it and one rival coupon makes Ownly the more
expensive app.

What it must fix first is **the restaurant list**. That's the only thing people in our research
refused to trade, and right now Ownly is offering 89% the same restaurants, 17 minutes slower.
Adding the local places people actually order from does more than any discount can.

Then fix **delivery reliability**, because reviews and interviews agree that failed orders are what
make people leave — not price. And treat **discounts as the last lever, not the first**: only 25% say
they'd stay once the offer ends, which is precisely how earlier challengers collapsed.

**In one line:** don't buy the first order — earn the second one, by stocking the restaurants people
actually want and delivering them properly.

**Things we could not check, said plainly**

  people *say* they'd do, not what they did.
