# B — Other Failed / Struggling Challengers to the Swiggy–Zomato Duopoly (India)

**Scope:** Challengers to the Indian food-delivery duopoly EXCEPT Uber Eats and Foodpanda-proper (covered in track A). Where Ola's ownership of Foodpanda India is relevant, it appears here.
**Access date for every URL in this document:** 2026-09-18
**Basis:** Secondary/desk research only. No primary research, no modelling, no estimation by the author.

---

## How to read the labels

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **FACT** | Documented in a filing, tribunal order, government statement, earnings call, or consistently reported across independent outlets |
| **MEDIA REPORT** | One or few publications, typically "sources said"; not independently verifiable |
| **COMPANY CLAIM** | Number originates with the company, its spokesperson, or a press release — even when quoted by a reputable outlet |
| **ANALYST ESTIMATE** | Brokerage / consultant model output, not an observation |
| **CONTESTED** | Sources disagree; both sides shown |
| **UNKNOWN** | Could not be sourced. Left blank rather than guessed |

> **Method warning applying to this whole document.** Indian startup metrics are overwhelmingly *single-upstream*. A figure appearing in ten outlets is usually one Moneycontrol / Entrackr / Inc42 scoop, re-syndicated. Where I could trace the upstream, I name it. Company claims are labelled as such even when a reputable publication carries them. **Several numbers commonly cited about Rapido's Ownly are estimates divided by estimates — see §6.3.**

---

## 0. Executive summary

Twelve years of evidence say the same thing four different ways:

1. **Low price is not a moat.** Every price-led challenger with observable before/after data lost volume when the subsidy stopped (ONDC −29% in four months; Thrive exited; Ola exited ONDC; Zepto Café −48%).
2. **The durable asset has always been the fleet, not the demand-side brand.** TinyOwl (consumer) died and Roadrunnr (logistics) was bought; Scootsy (consumer) was absorbed; Dunzo's brand affection did not survive its dark-store capex.
3. **Capital is necessary but nowhere near sufficient.** Amazon ($∞ balance sheet) stayed in one city for 31 months. Dunzo ($485M) reached insolvency. Ola committed $200M to Foodpanda and shut it in 17 months.
4. **Both incumbents publicly diagnose the 2026 wave as price-driven and structurally unsustainable** — Eternal's Deepinder Goyal (July 2026) and Swiggy's Rohit Kapoor (August 2026) — while Swiggy simultaneously runs its own low-price app, Toing. That contradiction is the most interesting live question in the category (§5, §7).

The cleanest natural experiment available anywhere in this dossier is **ONDC 2024–25**, where the subsidy was cut by a known amount on a known date and volumes are publicly tracked. See §4.1 and §7.

### ⚠️ Correction to the research brief
The brief dates the shutdown to **December 2023**. Every source located dates it to **29 December 2022**, announced 25 November 2022 (TechCrunch 2022-11-25; Business Standard 2022-11-25; Bloomberg 2022-11-28). **Treat "Dec 2023" as an error and do not reproduce it.** This matters because it changes the lifespan from ~3.5 years to ~2.6 years and puts the shutdown inside Amazon's 2022 global cost-cutting cycle rather than the 2023 one.

### Diagnosis — "why did a company with infinite capital fail?"

**The premise is wrong. Amazon never deployed the capital.** The observable facts:

- Amazon Food stayed in **one city for 31 months** and, per TechCrunch, Amazon "never heavily promoted or marketed the platform." [MEDIA REPORT — TechCrunch, 2022-11-25] Infinite balance-sheet capital ≠ capital *allocated*. Amazon Food is not evidence that money can't win Indian food delivery; it is evidence that **Amazon never ran the experiment at scale.**
- **It was killed by a global cost cycle, not by Swiggy/Zomato.** The shutdown was one of *three* India exits announced within a fortnight — Amazon Academy (edtech), Amazon Distribution (wholesale), Amazon Food — during Andy Jassy's company-wide cost-reduction campaign and layoffs. [FACT — Bloomberg 2022-11-28; TechCrunch 2022-11-27]
- **Amazon's broader India position was weakening.** Bloomberg noted Amazon lagging Walmart's Flipkart and struggling in smaller Indian cities; in April 2023 Amazon omitted its India business from its earnings disclosure for the first time in years. [MEDIA REPORT — Bloomberg 2022-11-28; TechCrunch 2023-04-27]

**Transferable lesson:** deep pockets are irrelevant if the parent's capital-allocation committee can delete the line item in a bad quarter. A challenger needs food delivery to be **core to its owner's identity**, not adjacent. Directly relevant to Rapido/Ownly (adjacent to core mobility, and answerable to a $3B valuation narrative) vs Swiggy/Toing (core).

| # | Attempt | Dates | Outcome | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Ola Cafe** | Mar 2015 – Mar 2016 | Shut after 12 months | contemporaneous press |
| 2 | **Foodpanda India** (acquired) | Dec 2017 – May/Jun 2019 | Ola committed **up to $200M**; suspended delivery May 2019, laid off most of ~**1,500 delivery executives** plus ~40 staff | BusinessToday 2019-05-22; TechCrunch 2019-05-21; Medianama 2019-05 |

This is the clearest documented instance of the **"cheap but unreliable"** failure mode: price was competitive, service was not, and the business did not survive.

### 2.5 Roadrunnr / Runnr — 2015 → 2017

| Field | Value | Label | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Model | Third-party hyperlocal **delivery-as-a-service** (a fleet, not a consumer food app) | FACT | TechCrunch |

- **Argument AGAINST Ownly:** Ola and Uber *also* owned fleets — and both failed at food delivery anyway. Medianama makes exactly this point (2026-03-05). Owning supply is **necessary but demonstrably not sufficient.**

### Diagnosis
- **Pivot failure, not category failure.** Dunzo's original business was *anything-delivery* (errands, peer-to-peer parcels) with genuine, unusual user affection. It pivoted to **Dunzo Daily** (10–19 min grocery dark stores) in 2021–22 into the teeth of Blinkit/Zepto/Instamart, and the dark-store capex destroyed it. [MEDIA REPORT — Rest of World; Outlook Business]
- **Food delivery specifically:** Dunzo never built a restaurant-aggregator business at scale; food was a *category inside* a general delivery app. **No credible orders/day figure for Dunzo food specifically was found.** [UNKNOWN]
- **Governance:** Reliance's stake reportedly carried veto powers that allegedly deterred new investors. [MEDIA REPORT / ALLEGED — Outlook Business; itechguides] Treat as allegation, not fact.
- **Marketing discipline:** IPL sponsorship spend is cited repeatedly as emblematic of brand spend layered on a negative-margin order. [MEDIA REPORT]
- Multiple co-founder exits, and CEO **Kabeer Biswas's** departure (to Flipkart Minutes), preceded the final shutdown. [FACT — widely reported]

**Transferable lesson:** the best-funded, most-loved challenger brand in Indian hyperlocal still lost roughly **₹8 for every ₹1 of revenue** and went to insolvency. Brand affection plus $485M did not produce a viable delivery P&L. **Nor did a strategic investor with infinite capital (Reliance) rescue it** — Reliance instead wrote the whole thing off. This is the second independent refutation (after Amazon) of the "deep-pocketed parent" thesis.

## 4. The "Zero-Commission / Lower Price" Wave — ONDC, magicpin, Thrive, DotPe, Waayu

### 4.1 ONDC — the cleanest natural experiment available

**Setup:** ONDC (government-backed open network, launched food delivery pilots Sept 2022 / scaled 2023) subsidised buyer apps to fund customer discounts. The subsidy was then cut by a known amount. Volumes are publicly tracked. This is as close to a controlled experiment as Indian food delivery offers.

| Date | Metric | Value | Label | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| June 2023 | Daily orders on ONDC (all retail) | ~30,000 "even with smaller incentives" | MEDIA REPORT | Business Standard, 2023-06-13 |
| May 2024 | Total transactions / retail orders | 8.9M total; ~5M retail | COMPANY CLAIM (ONDC data) | YourStory, June 2024 |
| **July 2024** | Monthly cap on per-buyer-app incentive | **₹3 crore** | MEDIA REPORT | Business Standard, 2025-03-27 |
| **Oct 2024** | Monthly retail orders — **PEAK** | **~6.5 million** | MEDIA REPORT | Business Standard, 2025-03-27 |
| **Feb 2025** | Monthly retail orders | **~4.6 million** | MEDIA REPORT | Business Standard, 2025-03-27 |
| ~Mar 2025 | Monthly incentive cap after cuts | **~₹30 lakh** (≈**−90%** from ₹3 cr) | MEDIA REPORT | Business Standard, 2025-03-27 |
| Jan–Aug 2025 | Total retail orders on network | **declined >5%** | MEDIA REPORT | Business Standard; cited in Medianama 2025-12-04 |
| 2025 (proposed) | **Re-introduction** of subsidies | **₹100–150 crore** earmarked for food-delivery firms | MEDIA REPORT | Inc42, 2025 |
| 2025 (announced) | Government corpus for restaurant incentives | **₹200–250 crore** | MEDIA REPORT | Inc42, 2025-08-04 |

> ### 📉 The headline finding
> **ONDC monthly retail orders fell ~29% (6.5M → 4.6M) in the four months after the per-buyer-app incentive cap was cut ~90% (₹3 cr → ₹30 lakh).**
> [MEDIA REPORT — Business Standard, 2025-03-27]

⚠️ **Three caveats any careful reader must apply:**

1. **These are retail orders across the whole network, not food-delivery orders specifically.** ONDC does not publish a clean monthly *food-delivery* series. **Do not present 6.5M → 4.6M as a food-delivery number.** (Mitigating note: ONDC itself states the majority of its retail orders come from the food & beverage category, so food is the dominant component — but the series is not food-only.)
2. **Single upstream.** The Business Standard piece of 2025-03-27 is the sole origin of the 6.5M/4.6M pair. Almost every later citation (Deccan Founders 2025-08, etc.) traces back to it. It is not corroborated by an independent measurement.
3. **Direction is nonetheless independently corroborated** — by Inc42 ("ONDC's entry… fizzled out with the network tapering off the discounts, and food delivery orders on the platform plateaued", 2025-08-04), by **Ola exiting** (Dec 2025), and above all by ONDC's own **revealed preference**: a network proposing ₹100–150 cr of *fresh* subsidies is a network that has concluded its volumes do not hold without them.

**Conflicting claim, shown for completeness and rejected:** a restaurant-industry vendor blog (dineopen.com, 2026) asserts ONDC food delivery orders "have been growing at 15–20% month-on-month through 2025–2026." This is an SEO/lead-gen blog with no stated methodology, and it contradicts Business Standard, Inc42 and Medianama. **Low credibility; do not cite.** [CONTESTED — weak side]

**Independent user-experience evidence:** LocalCircles survey — **54% of e-commerce users who tried ONDC found it cumbersome.** [SURVEY — localcircles.com] This is survey data with the usual self-selection caveats, but it points at the non-price failure: experience, not price.

**Structural diagnosis.** An anonymous former ONDC executive, via Inc42 (2025-12-21):
> *"Food delivery is not a pure marketplace business but a logistics, service reliability and trust product."*

Inc42's own forecast in the same piece: ONDC will **"fade in 2026 as a commercial disruptor"** and survive as a policy instrument. [MEDIA REPORT / ANALYST-STYLE, 2025-12-21]

### 4.6 The #OrderDirect movement — what actually happened

**Source: The Ken, "Eateries waged war to get back control from Zomato, Swiggy. Then, this happened."** (paywalled; the following is from The Ken's own public summary/morning-email teaser and from search-surfaced excerpts — **flagged as partially-verified**).

- As a result, restaurant owners **stopped relying on** the direct-ordering services.

[MEDIA REPORT — the-ken.com; article not fully accessible without subscription. **Treat the specific "15+/10+" counts as reported-but-unverified by me.** The direction is corroborated independently by Thrive's actual shutdown and by DotPe's pivot.]

**Why restaurants abandoned it — the mechanism that matters:** the restaurants did not leave because direct ordering was more expensive for *them*. It was far cheaper (1–3% vs 20–30%). They left because **the demand did not follow.** The bottleneck was never restaurant-side economics; it was **consumer habit and consumer expectation of discounts.** This is the single most transferable finding in §4 and it applies directly to every zero-commission pitch made to restaurants in 2026, including Ownly's.

### 6.7 The documented critical case against Ownly

1. **No disclosed monetisation.** Zero commission + ₹30 delivery + no ads/subscription = no identified path to contribution margin. [Medianama, 2026-03-05]
2. **Precedent.** Medianama explicitly notes that prior challengers **with logistics networks — Ola and Uber — failed anyway.** Owning a fleet was not sufficient for them. [Medianama, 2026-03-05]
3. **Mobility ≠ food economics.** Upstox notes a **fixed** ₹30 delivery fee against a **variable** delivery cost is structurally different from ride-hailing, where price scales with distance. A flat fee is a short-distance-order subsidy scheme that gets worse as the radius widens. [Upstox, 2026-04-06]
4. **Ecosystem stickiness.** Incumbents hold loyalty programmes, quick-commerce bundling and dining. Price alone does not dislodge a bundle. [Upstox, 2026-04-06]
5. **Analyst verdict.** JP Morgan judged Ownly the **weaker** of the two low-price entrants over Mar–Jun 2026. [Storyboard18, 2026-06-16]
6. **Neither incumbent's institutional research names it.** HDFC Securities' Swiggy update (2026-08-11) does not mention Rapido or Ownly once. [FACT — full-text verified]
7. **Both incumbents' CEOs publicly dismiss the model** (§7).
8. **Rapido's own framing is defensive on the cannibalisation point.** CEO **Aravind Sanka** frames Ownly as targeting the ~70,000 *offline* Bengaluru restaurants (of 100,000+ FSSAI-licensed, with <30,000 online on major platforms) — i.e. market *expansion*, not share capture. **This is the identical "incremental, not cannibalised" claim that JP Morgan flagged as the merely-optimistic case for Toing.** [COMPANY CLAIM — Upstox 2026-04-06]

### 6.8 The strongest honest arguments FOR Ownly

In fairness, the case is not one-sided:

1. **It owns the fleet.** §2.5 shows the fleet is the historically durable asset. Rapido operates bike taxis across ~600 cities — instant delivery supply without years of build. [MEDIA REPORT]
2. **Genuine restaurant-side grievance exists and is organised.** Inc42 (2026-07-30) documents Bengaluru restaurants pushing back on Swiggy with an **15 August deadline**, and Ownly signing an **MoU with NRAI**. NRAI president **Sagar Daryani**: *"Now, for that same customer who would come to you, you are paying 20% commission and having it delivered to their place. That's been the biggest shift."* Food delivery has gone from ~10% of restaurant business pre-pandemic to ~50% now. [MEDIA REPORT — Inc42, 2026-07-30]
3. **The incumbents took it seriously enough to act.** Swiggy divested a **₹2,399 crore** stake in Rapido explicitly over conflict of interest (Sept 2025) — an expensive, revealed-preference signal. [FACT]
4. **The reported trajectory is real growth**, whatever its cost: 5,000 → 50,000 daily orders in ~5 months. [MEDIA REPORT, single-upstream]

### 7.2 What the two incumbents say — on the record, in earnings calls

These are the highest-quality statements available on this question, because they are made under securities-disclosure discipline. **They are also self-interested.** Both apply.

**Eternal (Zomato), Q1 FY27 call, 22 July 2026 — Deepinder Goyal:**
> *"These platforms are offering the same restaurants, similar or longer delivery times, and lower menu prices funded by lower commissions and delivery fee — making the revenue gap even more unsustainable."*
> *"There's no new use case being unlocked here. **The customer traction is purely price-driven, and price-driven traction without structural economics tends to resolve itself.**"*
> On his own answer: *"What we are spending energy on is Bistro, which is our answer to the question these platforms are pretending to solve."*

Eternal also stated that **customer retention "remains strong" despite aggressive rival pricing**, and that the impact of Toing and Ownly has been **"limited."** [FACT — earnings call; ANI 2026-07-22; Medianama 2026-07; **COMPANY CLAIM as to the underlying retention data, which is not published**]

**Swiggy, Q1 FY27 call, 7 August 2026 — Rohit Kapoor (CEO, Food Marketplace):**
> *"If it is zero commission, then who pays for the cost of the platform? Somebody has to pay."*
> *"If you look at the lower AOVs and look at those costs, they do not really reflect a zero commission structure."*

**Sriharsha Majety (co-founder, Group CEO):**
> *"Commissions do need to be different from the Swiggy platform to enable our restaurant partners to offer everyday low prices."*

[FACT — earnings call, Medianama 2026-08-07]

> ### The most interesting contradiction in this whole dossier
> **Swiggy publicly argues zero-commission food delivery is arithmetically unsustainable — while simultaneously running Toing, its own low-price app, which it says will operate at ~2.5% margins.** Swiggy's position is that *lower* commission funded by a *lower cost structure* works, but *zero* commission does not. That is a coherent position, but it is a narrow ledge, and it is the same ledge Rapido claims to be standing on.

### 7.3 The one genuine piece of counter-evidence — and why it is weak

**Swiggy's claim, relayed by HDFC Securities (2026-08-11): "Two out of every three Toing users are either new or dormant food delivery users," and the remainder "tend to increase their combined usage across Swiggy's portfolio rather than simply substituting orders." Swiggy adds it "does not view affordability initiatives as heavily cannibalistic."**

This is the only substantive evidence found that a low-price proposition creates *incremental* rather than *shifted* demand. Reasons to discount it:

- It is a **COMPANY CLAIM relayed by a broker**, not an audited disclosure. Swiggy is not obliged to publish Toing cohort data and has not.
- It is **directly contradicted by JP Morgan** (2026-06-16), which flags cannibalisation as a live risk and attributes Toing's growth to performance marketing.
- HDFC Securities, which relayed the claim, **nonetheless modelled below management's guidance** (17% GOV CAGR vs 20–29% implied; ₹40bn adj. EBITDA vs ₹50bn targeted), writing: *"We suspect squeezing out both higher growth vs current pace along with 5% adj EBITDA is a difficult equation to solve."* **The broker repeated the claim and then declined to underwrite it.**
- It measures **acquisition** (are these users new?), not **retention** (do they stay when the discount stops?). **The retention question remains unanswered by this data.**

**magicpin's FY24** is the other apparent counter-example — 2.9x revenue growth with adjusted EBITDA improving from −39.2% to −9.8%. But **FY24 is the peak-subsidy year.** It cannot answer a post-subsidy question. FY25 numbers are missing.

### 7.4 Conclusion on the central question

1. **No challenger in the Indian record has demonstrated post-subsidy retention.** Not ONDC, not magicpin (unproven), not Thrive, not DotPe, not Waayu, not Ola.
2. **The consistent failure mode is not price — it is that price alone does not overcome habit, and the cheap challengers were worse at the thing that actually retains users: reliability.** Ola's ONDC exit (tracking failures, refunds, support) and the ex-ONDC-executive's line — *"food delivery is not a pure marketplace business but a logistics, service reliability and trust product"* — are the clearest articulations of this.
3. **The #OrderDirect episode is the purest test and the clearest result:** restaurants were offered 1–3% commission instead of 20–30%, and enthusiastically took it. **The consumers did not follow.** Supply-side economics were never the binding constraint.
4. **Ownly's position on this question is genuinely undetermined, not yet negative.** It differs from every predecessor in one material way — **it owns the fleet**, so it controls the reliability variable that ONDC buyer apps and direct-ordering SaaS never could. Its growth to a reported 50k/day is real. What is *not* known, and what nobody has published, is **whether any of those users stay when the ₹30 flat fee has to become a ₹60 flat fee, or when commission has to become non-zero.** No cohort or repeat data exists for Ownly in the public record.
