## How to read the labels

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **FACT** | From a primary/regulatory filing (SEC 10-K/10-Q/8-K, RoC/MCA filings, company annual report) |
| **MEDIA REPORT** | Reported by a named publication, sourced to unnamed insiders or the reporter's own reporting |
| **COMPANY CLAIM** | Statement by the company or its executives (press release, blog, interview) — treat as spin-prone |
| **ANALYST ESTIMATE** | Attributed to a research firm (RedSeer, Cowen, etc.) or a named analyst |
| **CONTESTED** | Credible sources disagree; both shown |
| **DERIVED** | Arithmetic performed by this author on FACT-grade figures; the inputs are cited |
| **UNKNOWN** | Could not be established from credible sources; deliberately left blank |

**Rule applied throughout:** no number appears here without a source. Where a widely-repeated number traces to a single upstream source, that is stated explicitly.

## A2. Hard numbers — Uber Eats India (FACT-grade, from Uber's SEC Form 8-K Ex-99.1, filed 21 Jan 2020)

This is the single most valuable source in the dossier: Uber published a standalone **"UBER EATS INDIA FINANCIAL AND OPERATIONAL HIGHLIGHTS"** table to let investors strip India out of Eats.

| Metric | Q1 2019 (Jan–Mar) | Q2 2019 (Apr–Jun) | Q3 2019 (Jul–Sep) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Revenue** | $2M | $6M | $20M |
| **Gross Bookings** | $93M | $110M | $103M |
| **Trips (orders)** | 38M | 42M | 37M |
| **MAPCs** (monthly active platform consumers) | 3M | 3M | 2M |
| **Operating loss** | **$(97)M** | **$(86)M** | **$(61)M** |
| **Adjusted EBITDA** | $(94)M | $(82)M | $(59)M |

**Label: FACT.** Source: Uber Technologies Form 8-K, Exhibit 99.1, filed 21 January 2020.

### Derived unit economics (arithmetic on the FACT table above)

| Derived metric | Q1 2019 | Q2 2019 | Q3 2019 | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **AOV (Gross Bookings ÷ Trips)** | **$2.45** | **$2.62** | **$2.78** | ≈ ₹170–₹200 at ~₹71/USD |
| **Operating loss per order** | **$(2.55)** | **$(2.05)** | **$(1.65)** | ≈ ₹181 → ₹117 per order |
| **Adj. EBITDA loss per order** | $(2.47) | $(1.95) | $(1.59) | |
| **Revenue as % of Gross Bookings ("take rate")** | **2.2%** | **5.5%** | **19.4%** | The Q3 jump is the monetisation push |
| **Orders/day (Trips ÷ days in quarter)** | **~422k/day** | **~462k/day** | **~402k/day** | |

**Label: DERIVED** (inputs are FACT). These are among the very few *auditable* cash-burn-per-order figures that exist for any Indian food-delivery player.

**The headline finding:** in Q1 2019 Uber Eats India was **losing roughly the same amount per order ($2.55) as the entire order was worth ($2.45)**. It was burning slightly more than 100% of gross bookings.

## A3. Stated reasons for failure — Uber Eats India

### Company spin (COMPANY CLAIM)

| Source | Statement |
|---|---|
| Uber Form 8-K, 21 Jan 2020 | Divestiture reflects *"our commitment to take a hard look at Eats markets where we do not have a path to leadership"* and to redeploy investment to markets with a clearer path to a top position. **This is the official reason: no path to #1 or #2.** |
| Dara Khosrowshahi (Uber CEO), press release 20 Jan 2020 | *"Our Uber Eats team in India has achieved an incredible amount over the last two years, and I couldn't be prouder of their ingenuity and dedication."* — pure praise, no diagnosis. |
| Uber press release, 20 Jan 2020 | Uber will now concentrate on its India **ride-sharing** business, described as *"the clear category leader."* |
| Deepinder Goyal (Zomato CEO), press release 20 Jan 2020 | *"We are proud to have pioneered restaurant discovery and to have created a leading food delivery business across more than 500 cities in India."* |

### Acquirer's framing (COMPANY CLAIM — Zomato, Inc42 23 Jan 2020)

- Goyal: *"The idea is to only improve the quality of our business and Uber Eats was a step in that direction. **It was not about buying a number three player**, but buying a number one or number two in some cities where we were lagging behind."*
- Expected gain: **+20% monthly active users**, **+25% orders**; Zomato expected to retain **~80%** of Uber Eats' business.
- **500 Uber Eats exclusive restaurants** being onboarded.
- Goyal framed the ~10% dilution as a *"win-win"* to gain 20%.
- Goyal claimed Zomato's **AOV was higher and discounts lower** than Swiggy's.

> **Note the tension:** Goyal publicly says Uber Eats was valuable *for its southern-India city positions and its exclusive restaurants*, not for national scale. That is an implicit statement that Uber Eats had **no national franchise worth buying** — only pockets.

### Analyst / journalist diagnosis (ANALYST ESTIMATE / MEDIA REPORT)

| Diagnosis | Source |
|---|---|
| **Structural: Indian AOV is too low to support delivery economics.** India delivery items average **$4** vs **$33** in US markets. | TechCrunch, 3 Mar 2020 |
| **Subsidy trap:** *"the food delivery firms have little choice but to keep subsidizing the cost of food items on their platform, as otherwise most of their customers can't afford them"* — **Anand Lunia, India Quotient (VC)** | TechCrunch, 3 Mar 2020 |
| **Duopoly already formed:** Zomato + Swiggy held ~80%; both were themselves losing **$15M+/month**. Entering as #3 into a two-horse subsidy war. | TechCrunch, 3 Mar 2020; Forbes India, 21 Jan 2020 |
| **Disproportionate loss contribution:** 3% of Eats gross bookings, >25% of Eats losses. | Forbes India, 21 Jan 2020 |
| **No brand or loyalty built:** despite aggressive discounting, Uber Eats "was unable to establish a strong brand or loyalty with customers and restaurants in India." | Secondary case-study sources — **low confidence, needs a better citation (Pass 2)** |
| **Uber's global playbook:** exit any market where #1/#2 is unreachable — same pattern as China (Didi), Russia (Yandex), Southeast Asia (Grab). | Quartz India, "If Uber's global record is anything to go by, it was bound to exit" |
| **Abrupt shutdown damaged supply-side trust:** restaurants and delivery partners were given no advance notice. Restaurant owner Hemendra Singh Rathore: *"We received no official intimation from Uber Eats either—all I got was an email."* Delivery partner Narayan Venkatesh Raju: *"Support team told me this is fake news and the issue will be resolved within an hour."* | Forbes India, 21 Jan 2020 (Pankti Mehta Kadakia, Naandika Tripathi) |

## B3. Stated reasons for failure — Foodpanda India

### Company spin (COMPANY CLAIM)

- **Ola, 27 May 2019 (official statement):** *"As part of our ongoing business repurposing initiatives, we are focused on building a portfolio of own food brands and curated food offerings through our fast expanding network of kitchens."*
  - **Note:** Ola **explicitly denied** it was shutting the delivery business, framing it as "repurposing." MediaNama's headline reflects the dispute: *"Update: Ola denies shutting Foodpanda's delivery biz, says it's 'repurposing' business to focus on kitchens."* This is the clearest company-spin-vs-reality contrast in the dossier.
- **Bhavish Aggarwal (Ola CEO), 10 Sep 2018:** *"The milestone that we have achieved in such a short span of time is a testament to our passion and commitment towards creating the best food experience for our customers."* — said 8 months before the business was gutted.

### Analyst / journalist diagnosis

| Diagnosis | Source |
|---|---|
| **"Good at acquisitions, bad at managing them"** — Ola's core failure was post-acquisition management, not the purchase | Quartz India headline, 2019 |
| **Pure discount-bought demand with no retention:** orders went 200k/day → 5k/day (a **~97.5% collapse**) once discounts were withdrawn. Demand was rented, never owned. | Quartz India, 2019; Entrackr |
| **Over-reliance on parent distribution:** the strategy leaned on the Ola app's **"150 million additional customers"** — cross-selling cab users into food. It did not convert into durable food-delivery habit. | Ola press release 10 Sep 2018 (the claim) vs the 5k/day outcome |
| **Pre-existing supply-side rot inherited from Rocket Internet era:** fake restaurant listings, unreliable deliveries, 300 layoffs in 2015 | Secondary press |
| **Fourth place in a two-player market:** competing against Swiggy/Zomato at 30M orders/month while doing ~3M at peak | MediaNama, 27 May 2019 |
| **Structurally broken unit economics:** delivery cost alone (₹267 Cr) was 3.3× revenue in FY19 | FACT (RoC) |
| **Serial ownership churn:** Rocket Internet → Delivery Hero (Dec 2016) → Ola (Dec 2017) → wind-down (May 2019). Three owners in 30 months. | Multiple |

## B5. Foodpanda India — the actual death date [PASS 3]

The "Foodpanda died in 2019" framing is imprecise. The correct sequence is a **three-stage death**:

| Stage | Date | What happened | Label | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Marketplace delivery business suspended** | **21–27 May 2019** | 1,500 delivery executives' contracts terminated, ~40 staff cut. **Ola publicly denied a shutdown**, calling it "repurposing" | MEDIA REPORT + COMPANY CLAIM | TechCrunch 21 May 2019; MediaNama 27 May 2019; Business Insider India, "Ola denies shutting down Foodpanda, says it has a refocused approach to food delivery" |
| **2. Zombie phase as cloud-kitchen brand** | May 2019 – Jul 2022 | Survives only as private-label cloud kitchens under **Ola Foods** (FLRT, The Great Khichdi Experiment, Lovemade, Grandma's Kitchen), 50+ kitchens, 5 cities | MEDIA REPORT | MediaNama 27 May 2019; TechCrunch 21 May 2019 |

**So: Foodpanda India was commercially dead in May 2019 and formally buried in July 2022** — a 38-month zombie period during which the brand existed but the food-delivery business did not.

### Verdict on the "3% / 25%" claim

**PARTIALLY VERIFIED / MIXED DENOMINATORS.** The claim as reported by Forbes India (21 Jan 2020) is **directionally correct and reproducible**, but only if the two halves use **different denominators**: the 3% works against full-year global Eats gross bookings, while the >25% requires the Q1–Q3 loss denominator. Cite it as **"roughly 3% of Uber's global Eats gross bookings but somewhere between a fifth and a third of its global Eats losses"** rather than repeating "3% / 25%" as a clean pair.

**Label: DERIVED from FACT inputs (Uber 8-K Ex-99.1 + Uber FY2019 earnings release), corroborating a MEDIA REPORT (Forbes India).**

### The under-appreciated FACT from the same release

Uber disclosed that **removing India would have lifted its global Eats take rate to 10.1% in Q4 2019**. India was not merely a loss-making region — it was **materially depressing Uber's headline global Eats monetisation metric**. This is Uber's own framing, in a regulated investor communication, and it is the strongest available evidence that the India exit was driven by **metric contamination as much as by absolute losses**.
