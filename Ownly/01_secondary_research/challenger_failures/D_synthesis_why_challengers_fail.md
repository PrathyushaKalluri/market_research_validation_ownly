## 1. Headline

Six challengers have attacked Indian food delivery with a lower price. **All six failed, and the
record contains no counter-example.** The common cause is not capital, technology or supply — three
of the six had more of all three than Ownly does. It is that **a price advantage is a reason to try
and not a reason to return**, and every one of them ran out of subsidy before it found a second reason.

The single most important line for the deck comes from the incumbent, unprompted, naming Ownly:

> *"These platforms are offering the same restaurants, similar or longer delivery times, and lower
> menu prices funded by lower commissions and delivery fee — making the revenue gap even more
> unsustainable. **There's no new use case being unlocked here.** The customer traction is purely
> price-driven, and price-driven traction without structural economics tends to resolve itself."*
> — Deepinder Goyal, Eternal Ltd Q1FY27 shareholders' letter, 22 Jul 2026 **[FACT — primary filing]**

**Our own Gachibowli audit independently confirms two of the three factual premises in that sentence**
(§3, M3). That is uncomfortable, and it is the most defensible finding this project currently holds.

## 2. The five failure mechanisms

### M1 — Rented demand: acquisition funded by discount, not by structure

| Evidence | Value | Label |
|---|---|---|
| **Foodpanda India** daily orders, Aug 2018 → mid-2019, after discounts stopped | **~200,000 → ~5,000 (−97.5%)** | MEDIA REPORT |
| Foodpanda FY19: revenue / loss / discounts / delivery charges | ₹82 Cr / ₹756 Cr / ₹137 Cr (1.67× revenue) / ₹267 Cr (3.3× revenue) | FACT (RoC filings) |
| **ONDC** monthly retail orders, Oct 2024 → Feb 2025, after per-buyer-app incentive cap cut ~90% (₹3 cr → ₹30 lakh) | **6.5M → 4.6M (−29%)** | MEDIA REPORT (single upstream, network-wide retail not food-only) |
| ONDC response: proposed fresh subsidies | ₹100–150 cr | MEDIA REPORT |
| **Uber Eats India** operating loss per order, Q1/Q2/Q3 2019 | **$2.55 / $2.05 / $1.65** against an AOV of $2.45–2.78 | **FACT (SEC Form 8-K Ex. 99.1, 21 Jan 2020)** |

In Q1 2019 **Uber Eats India lost more per order than the order was worth** — burn above 100% of
gross bookings. ONDC's subsidy proposal is revealed preference: the volumes do not hold on their own.

**Diagnostic:** demand bought with a discount has a half-life measured in weeks after the discount stops.

### M2 — Attacking the side of the market that was never scarce

Zero commission wins restaurants. Restaurants were never the constraint.

| Evidence | Value | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Zomato to the CCI: share of its restaurants that are **exclusive** | **~1%** (∴ ~99% already multi-home) | FACT (CCI submission) |
| CCI Case 16/2021 (4 Apr 2022): "exorbitant commissions" | **Expressly NOT sent for investigation** | FACT |
| **Thrive** — 3% commission, Jubilant FoodWorks 35% + Coca-Cola 15% shareholders | Consumer app **shut Dec 2024**; FY23 revenue ₹2.5 cr, loss ₹7.4 cr | MEDIA REPORT |
| **DotPe** — loudest #OrderDirect campaigner | Survived by **pivoting to POS software that plugs restaurants *into* Swiggy/Zomato** | MEDIA REPORT |

Restaurants don't switch — they **add**. The challenger buys a restaurant list nearly identical to the
incumbent's and, to buy it, deletes its own largest revenue line. The Ken's mechanism: restaurants
adopted 1–3% commission happily; **consumers didn't follow.**

**There is no regulatory tailwind.** A zero-commission play cannot count on the CCI forcing
incumbent take rates down.

### M3 — No new use case: same restaurants, same or slower

This is Goyal's diagnosis (§1) and it is the mechanism that converts M1 and M2 into failure. If the
assortment is the same and the service is worse, price is the *only* differentiator — which is M1.

### M4 — The reliability floor: cheap but broken

| Evidence | Label |
|---|---|
| **Ola exited ONDC Dec 2025** after user complaints of tracking failures, refund delays, dead support | MEDIA REPORT |
| Eternal disclosed cohort retention: **Q4 46% → Q12 49%**; NOV retention **279% by Q12**, held *"despite aggressive pricing from competitors"* | **FACT (Q1FY27 letter)** |

The incumbent's retention *rose* with cohort age through a price war. Cheapness did not move it.

### M5 — A revenue architecture too thin to survive the counter-attack

| Platform | Revenue per order | Label |
|---|---|---|
| **Swiggy food delivery** — Adjusted Revenue ÷ GOV (bundles commission + advertising + customer delivery fee + platform fee) | **24.0% (FY22) → 25.6% (FY26)** | FACT (corporate deck) + CALCULATED, corroborated by HSIE |
| **Ownly (stated)** | ~₹30 delivery fee, **0% commission**, no subscription, no ads found | MEDIA REPORT / COMPANY CLAIM |
| **Ownly (observed, Gachibowli, 2026-09-16)** | **₹0 delivery fee in 8 of 8 rows; ₹0 platform; ₹0 packaging; GST only** | **FACT (our audit)** |

> **⚠ Take rate is contested and the figures are not comparable.** HSIE computes Swiggy food delivery at
> **25.6% (FY26)**; HSBC (as cited) puts Swiggy at **21.9%** and Zomato at **24.4%**. The denominators
> differ (Adj.Rev÷GOV vs Adj.Rev÷NOV vs B2C blended). **An earlier draft of this file cited "~14.8%
> blended take rate"; that figure could not be traced to a primary filing in the follow-up pass and has
> been withdrawn.** Quote a take rate only with its denominator attached.

**What a challenger must actually fund** — the most useful numbers in the whole dossier, because they
are disclosed in rupees-equivalent terms and FACT-grade (Swiggy DRHP pp. 203, 358):

| Swiggy food delivery, % of GOV | FY22 | FY23 | FY24 | Q1FY25 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **Cost of delivery** | 14.59% | 14.59% | 13.86% | **14.31%** |
| Delivery fees collected **from users** | 5.98% | 5.47% | 4.08% | **3.11%** |
| **Contribution margin** | 1.59% | 2.94% | 5.72% | **6.40%** |
| Adjusted EBITDA | (7.63%) | (4.81%) | (0.19%) | **0.85%** |

**INTERPRETATION (ours):** delivery costs ~14% of GOV and the *incumbent* recovers only ~3% of it from
the customer — the gap is funded by commission, advertising and platform fee. Ownly has deleted the
commission line, and on our observed rows was charging the customer nothing either. Note also the
direction of travel: incumbents have been **cutting** the customer-facing delivery fee as a share of
GOV (5.98% → 3.11%) while raising the platform fee — i.e. they have already been re-architecting
*which* line the customer pays, which is the same move Ownly is selling as a differentiator.

**A disclosure change that removes the evidence:** Eternal now reports only NOV (**NOV = GOV minus
discounts**) and **sunset GOV from Q1FY27**. The GOV–NOV gap was the only public proxy for
restaurant-funded discounting; it is now permanently unobservable. **FACT**, and a defensible
analytical point for the deck.

**Platform fee rose >645% in 31 months (₹2 → ₹14.90), and Swiggy matched Zomato's March 2026 hike
within 4 days** — tacit price leadership, and roughly **₹15/order of pure headroom** the incumbents
can surrender to compress a challenger's advantage while still out-earning it on take rate alone.

**INTERPRETATION (ours, flagged):** on our observed Gachibowli rows, Ownly's revenue per order from
both sides of the market was **zero**. Whatever the steady state is, it is not what we measured.

### The prize, for scale

> *"Our first annual $10 billion in NOV took 18 years… it took **16 years** to reach Adjusted EBITDA
> profitability in FY24."* — Eternal Q1FY27 **[FACT]**

Current food-delivery margins: **Eternal 5.6% of NOV (₹606 Cr), Swiggy 3.1% of GOV (₹292 Cr)**, with
guidance capped at 5–6%. Sixteen years of duopoly for a mid-single-digit margin.

## 3. Ownly scored against each mechanism — using our own data

| # | Mechanism | Ownly's position | Our evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **M1** | Rented demand | **PARTIALLY ESCAPED — the strongest thing Ownly has.** Its price gap is *structural* (fees removed), not discount-funded. Non-food share of bill: **Ownly 4.8% (tax only) vs Zomato 15.1%, Swiggy 27.4%.** No other challenger in the record had this. **But:** incumbent coupons still erased it — Ownly cheapest **4/4 on list+fees (median −30.5%)**, only **2/4 after each app's auto-applied offer (median +13.9% dearer)**. A ₹156 Swiggy coupon turned a ₹205.80 Ownly order into ₹45. | Audit slot 1 |
| **M2** | Non-scarce side | **REPEATING THE PATTERN.** Cross-platform overlap **8/9 = 89%** (K42). Ownly bought substantially the same list. | Audit K42 |
| **M3** | No new use case | **CONFIRMED BY OUR OWN MEASUREMENT.** Same restaurants (89% overlap) **and ~17 min slower** (median ETA 39.5 min vs Swiggy 22.5, Zomato 17.5). Goyal's "same restaurants, similar or longer delivery times" is *empirically true of Ownly in Gachibowli*, measured by us, not asserted by him. | Audit §4, §5 |
| **M4** | Reliability floor | **AT RISK.** Fulfilment failure = **47% of first-hand accounts** and **72% of app-store reviews**; support/refund 28% / 72%. Incumbents recovered failed orders better in **3 of 3** direct comparisons. | Consolidated pack |
| **M5** | Thin revenue architecture | **MOST EXPOSED.** Observed revenue/order ₹0 vs incumbents' take rate + ₹17.58 + subscription + ads, with ~₹15/order of counter-attack headroom. | Audit §3 + filings |

**Read across the row:** Ownly is genuinely different on **M1 only**. On M2, M3 and M5 it is running the
same play that failed six times, and M4 is the mechanism that historically converts that into churn.

## 4. Where Ownly can actually do better

Ranked by *defensibility against a competitor who can outspend it*. Each is a **hypothesis for the
survey/interviews**, not a recommendation we have earned yet.

### 4.1 Lean on the ONE thing the incumbents cannot copy in a quarter — supply they don't have

The audit found exactly one differentiated item, and it matters more than its size suggests:
**Aanimuthyalu Unlimited — on Ownly only, absent from both incumbents.** Meanwhile **Pizza Hut is on
both incumbents and not on Ownly**, consistent with the Bengaluru KFC/McDonald's delisting.
Local coverage **3/3 = 100%** vs chain **4/5 = 80%** (K41, **+20pp**).

**The argument:** "same restaurants, cheaper" is precisely the diagnosis that killed the other six.
"Restaurants you cannot get anywhere else" is a *new use case* — the thing Goyal says is missing —
and it is the only asset a zero-commission model is structurally better at acquiring, because the
long tail of sub-₹150 local eateries is exactly who cannot afford a 15–25% take rate. Ownly already
says local/regional eateries are its target; our audit says its actual Gachibowli catalogue is
**showcase chains**. That gap is the strategic finding.
→ **Audit gap to close:** every Ownly price we hold is a chain. `thu_lunch` must capture Murgan
Tiffins and Mehfil — the under-₹150 local eateries Ownly claims to target.

### 4.2 Make the structural saving legible — users currently cannot see it

Ownly's advantage is the *absence* of fees, and absence is invisible. Our own corpus shows users
cannot state what Ownly's delivery fee even is: described as free (3), "+5 rupees" (1), ₹15 (1),
"₹40 extra" (2), against a media/company figure of ₹30 flat. **Nine items explicitly prefer a
clearly-labelled flat fee** to "free delivery" they don't trust.

A saving nobody can articulate cannot be remembered at the next order — which is how a structural
advantage decays into M1 behaviour.

### 4.3 Treat reliability as the retention instrument, not a cost line

Eternal's disclosed cohorts (46% → 49%, NOV retention 279% by Q12) held *through* the price war. The
project's own pre-registered weighting already reflects this — **D4 Experience carries the highest
weight (25%)** and any dimension < 40 blocks SCALE. §2/M4 says D4 is where Ownly is weakest.
The ~17-minute ETA gap is the specific, measurable form of it, and survey Q16 (₹30 cheaper vs 15 min
slower) tests exactly that trade.

### 4.4 Close the payment gaps — cheapest differentiation available

**No meal-card (Pluxee) support** and **no cash on delivery**. Gachibowli is a salaried-IT catchment;
meal cards are a live, concrete reason a professional cannot order even when they want to. Zero
strategic cost to fix, and it is a *use case*, not a price cut.

### 4.5 Distribution via Rapido — real, but not the differentiator it looks like

Rapido gives ~74M MAU (national) and an amortised rider fleet. **Caution, and it is a serious one:
Uber Eats India was exactly this play** — ride-hailing fleet amortised into food delivery — and it
sold for **$206M** (Uber 10-K; the "$350M" in Indian media appears in no SEC filing) after losing more
per order than the order was worth. **Amazon Food, Foodpanda/Ola and Uber Eats all had parent
distribution. None of them was saved by it.** Distribution reduces CAC; it does not create a use case.

Also two-sided in our data: Rapido ride grievances appear on Ownly's own launch post, and users
predict "the Rapido playbook." H7's brand-blind → brand-reveal test must be allowed to come back negative.

## 7. What this does NOT establish

- **Nothing about Hyderabad customers.** Hyderabad appears 4 times in 524 social items. Every failure
  case here is national or Bengaluru.
- **Nothing about Ownly's actual economics.** Burn per order, CAC and CLV are UNKNOWN and are not
  estimated anywhere in this file, per the standing rule.
- **Six failures is a pattern, not a law.** Ownly's structural (non-promotional) price gap is a real
  difference in kind from Foodpanda's discounting, and the record has no prior case of a challenger
  with a fee-structure advantage *plus* an amortised rider fleet *plus* a 74M-MAU distribution channel.
  The honest statement is: **the mechanism that killed the others is present in Ownly's Gachibowli
  operation on three of five dimensions, and our instruments can measure whether it binds.**
- **Our audit is n = 4 complete three-app comparisons**, one slot, one address, with Ownly on a new
  account and the incumbents on subscribed accounts. Directional.
