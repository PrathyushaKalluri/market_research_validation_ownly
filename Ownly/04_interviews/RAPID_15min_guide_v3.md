# Rapid Interview Guide — 15 minutes (v3, LIVE)

**Owner: Person 2. Target: 8 interviews (4 students · 4 working professionals), ≥5 in-catchment.**
Compressed from `interview_guide_30min.md`. Consent text: `consent_and_privacy.md`. Screener: `screener.md`.
**Do not start before written ethics approval.**

## The two jobs this guide does
1. **Feed Service Q.** The first two interviews decide whether the Hyderabad-localized proposition
   (`13_survey_v3_live/survey_questions.md` §7 and fake-door variant B) is describing the right thing.
   **These two must happen before the form is shared.**
2. **Explain the survey numbers.** Everything after interview 2 is the *why* behind the percentages.

## Rules
- **Recent behaviour first, opinion last.** Never ask "would you like X?" when "tell me about the last
  time you X" is available.
- **Do not say "Ownly" or "Rapido" before §5.** If they say it first, follow them, and note that they did.
- **Never explain, defend or sell anything.** You are not testing whether they like an idea; you are
  finding out what they already do.
- Silence is a tool. Count to three before filling a gap.
- Notes, not recordings, unless they explicitly consent to a recording.

## §0 · Open (1 min)
> "Thanks for the time. We're students doing a course project on how people around here pick food-delivery apps. There are no right answers and I'm not selling anything. It takes about 15 minutes. I'll take written notes, nothing is recorded, and your name won't appear anywhere. You can skip anything or stop whenever. Is that okay?"

☐ Verbal consent recorded · ☐ Age 20–30 confirmed · ☐ Ordered delivery in last 4 weeks confirmed

## §1 · The last order (3 min) — *the anchor for everything after*
1. "Tell me about the **last** food-delivery order you placed. Walk me through it from the moment you decided to order."
2. *(probe)* "What made you open that app rather than another one?"
3. *(probe)* "Do you remember what it came to at the end?" → "Did that feel about right?"
4. *(probe)* "Was there anything at checkout you weren't expecting?"

> Listen for: whether price came up **unprompted**, and whether it came up as *amount* or as *surprise*.
> Write down which. That distinction is the whole A-vs-B question.

## §2 · The last order that went wrong (3 min)
5. "When was the last time a delivery order went badly? What happened?"
6. *(probe)* "What did you do about it?" → "How did that end?"
7. "Did it change how you order since?" → *(if yes)* "How exactly?"
8. "Roughly how many bad orders would it take before you stopped using an app?"

> Listen for: whether the consequence was *stopped using it*, *complained*, or *nothing*. If failures
> reliably produce "nothing", the reliability proposition is weaker than our secondary data suggests —
> and that would be a real finding worth reporting.

## §3 · What they'd give up (3 min)
9. "If an app saved you about **₹30** on a typical order but took **15 minutes longer** — which do you take?"
10. "Same ₹30 saving, but it's late about **3 times in 10** instead of **1 in 10**?"
11. "Same ₹30 saving, but it only has **a few** of your usual places?"
12. "How much cheaper would something have to be, **every single time**, before you actually switched over?"

> After each: **"why?"** — the reason matters more than the choice. One line, verbatim, in their words.

## §4 · The two ideas — *brand-blind* (3 min)
Show the printed card (`proposition_card_v3.md`, one side each). Let them read. Do not narrate.

13. "If only one of these existed where you live, which would you start using?"
14. "**Which line on that card made you pick it?**" ← *write this verbatim, it is the single highest-value quote in the interview*
15. "What's missing from the one you picked?"
16. "Do you think prices like that last, once an app gets popular?"

> **For interviews 1 and 2 only, also ask:** "If you were writing the second card for someone in
> Hyderabad — what would you put on it that isn't there?" Then bring the answer straight back to the team.
> That answer is what Service Q's bullets should say.

## §5 · The reveal (2 min)
> "The second one is roughly how a service called **Ownly** describes itself — it's from **Rapido**, the bike-taxi app."

17. "Had you heard of it?" → *(if yes)* "Where from?"
18. "Does it being Rapido change anything for you?" → "Which way, and why?"
19. *(if they've ordered on it)* "Tell me about that order." → "Would you use it again with **no** discount on the next three orders?"
20. *(if they haven't)* "What's actually stopped you?"

## §6 · Close (1 min)
21. "Last one — what would make you switch your main food app tomorrow?"
22. "Anything I should have asked and didn't?"
> "That's everything — thank you. If you know one or two people around here aged 20–30 who order a lot, could you pass on our survey link?"

☐ Survey link shared · ☐ Snowball referrals asked for

## Write-up — within 10 minutes of finishing, while it's fresh

Fill one row in `participant_tracker_v3.csv` and one block in `interview_notes_v3_template.md`.
**Five things, nothing else, if you're short on time:**
1. The last-order story in 2 lines.
2. The went-wrong story in 2 lines, and what they did about it.
3. The 4 trade-off answers (Q9–Q12) and the ₹ switching number.
4. **The verbatim from Q14.**
5. Your one-line read: *did price or reliability drive this person?*

## Coding
Use the existing families in `coding_framework.md`, mapped to the same 8 families as the secondary data
(`01_secondary_research/consolidated/consolidated_evidence_pack.md` §1) so interviews, reviews and social
posts can sit in one triangulation table: PRICE_ADVANTAGE · PRICE_DOUBT · FULFILMENT_FAIL ·
SUPPORT_REFUND · ASSORTMENT · COMPETITION · DISTRIBUTION · TRUST_QUALITY.

**Reporting rule: n=8 is 8 people.** Report counts ("6 of 8 said…"), never percentages, and never
"users think". Interviews explain the survey; they do not measure anything.
