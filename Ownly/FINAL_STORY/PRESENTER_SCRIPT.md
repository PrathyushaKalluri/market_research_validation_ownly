# Presenter script — 7 minutes, 10 screens (+ evidence page as backup)

About 40 seconds a screen. Say the headline first, point at the one highlighted bar, then give one line of "so what".
Every number below is on the screen and in the evidence page. If asked "how did you get that?", go to page 11 and read the row.

---

**1 · The problem (45s)**
"Ownly is Rapido's food app: no commission for restaurants, no platform fee for customers. In Bengaluru it reached
50,000 orders a day in a year. But look at *why*: in July 2026 over a thousand restaurants threatened to leave Swiggy
and Zomato, and Ownly was the door they walked through. Hyderabad has no such revolt. So our question was not
'should Ownly enter' — it's already live — but **which parts of the Bengaluru playbook still work here.**"

**2 · Hyderabad users (40s)**
"We surveyed 40 people in the Gachibowli catchment, mostly students aged 20–24. 55% of their orders go to Swiggy, 37% to
Zomato. **82.5% already pay for a membership, almost half pay for both.** And the typical person needs ₹30 off every order
to switch."

**3 · Our KPIs (45s)**
"Because the question is 'does the playbook transfer', we tracked the funnel — heard, opened, ordered — and the four
barriers that could break it. **17 of 40 had heard of Ownly, 7 opened it, 2 ordered.** The two blue bars are the big
barriers: rival memberships and the belief that prices will rise. And Rapido users were *less* aware of Ownly than
non-users."

**4 · Marketing metrics (40s)**
"Our main metric is switch-threshold coverage: for each person and each basket, is Ownly's real saving at least what
*that person* said they need? On menu price, yes 81% of the time. With a rival membership, 56%. **After one rival coupon,
27%.** From the formula sheet, penetration share is 2 of 36. Retention, CLV and CAC we could not compute — we show why."

**5 · Market & competitors (45s)**
"Our price audit: Ownly was cheaper in 4 of 4 baskets on menu price — by ₹114 — but ₹28 *dearer* after a rival coupon.
**8 of 9 restaurants are the same ones on Swiggy or Zomato, and Ownly quotes 17 minutes longer.** Six earlier price-led
challengers all failed. Zomato's founder said it himself: 'no new use case'. The difference: Ownly's saving comes from
removing fees, not from paying for discounts."

**6 · Customers (45s)**
"This is the key finding. For ₹30, 92.5% would wait longer, 72.5% would accept late orders, but **only 35% would give up
their restaurants.** Our fake door confirmed it in behaviour: when the place they wanted wasn't there, **16 of 30 went back
to their usual app or didn't order.** Price gets people to try; missing restaurants make them leave."

**7 · Trust (40s)**
"The closer people get to a real order, the worse it sounds: failed orders come up in 72% of app reviews, half of posts by
people who ordered, and a fifth of general chatter. In the fake door, **18 of 34 took cash now because they may not come
back or don't trust later offers.**"

**8 · Fake door (45s)**
"We built a working app: 70 live sessions. Two surprises. **45% paid ₹15–49 for Rapido Link**, 25 minutes instead of 42 — and
higher prices didn't put them off, even though the survey said people would wait. And 62% accepted a ₹15 per-order fee,
while only 8 of 42 chose a monthly membership. The top worry: 'the captain will drop a passenger first'."

**9 · Propositions (45s)**
"So: **keep** zero commission and no fees. **Adapt**: stop saying 'cheaper than Swiggy' and go deep on the local restaurants
people won't give up. **Add** Rapido Link — paid fast delivery with no passenger on board — and same-day refund cover.
**Deprioritise** big first-order discounts and a third membership. Don't buy the first order; earn the second one."

**10 · UI (30s)**
"Three screens: a home that leads with your regular restaurants and asks for missing ones; a delivery choice that sells
speed and answers the passenger worry; and a bill that shows every line instead of a comparison a coupon can disprove."

---

**If asked about the fake-door A/B test:** "We dropped the discount-vs-restaurants comparison. The app fixed the arm per
phone, and shared phones gave 58 vs 12 sessions, so it isn't a fair comparison. We removed 25 test rows too."

**If asked about sample size:** "Everything under 30 people is labelled 'small' and read as direction; under 10 we show
counts only. It's all on the evidence page with 95% intervals."
