# A. Research Charter: Ownly Early Market-Fit in Gachibowli (v2)

**Version:** v2, 2026-09-14. This replaces the v1 "should Ownly enter Hyderabad?" framing in root files `13_`–`17_`. Those files are kept for reference only.
**Owner:** Research lead. **Status:** Pre-fieldwork. No primary data has been collected yet.

---

## 1. Refined problem statement

> **Evaluate Ownly's early Hyderabad market fit by identifying whether its Bengaluru value proposition transfers to Gachibowli, and quantify unmet food-delivery needs, switching triggers, price–reliability trade-offs, willingness-to-pay and adoption barriers among 20–30-year-old students and working professionals.**

**Why the reframe matters.** Ownly is already operating and marketing in Hyderabad (user-supplied context; Agent A is verifying launch date and service areas in `01_secondary_research/`). So the study does not ask whether to enter. It asks:

1. whether the reasons Ownly worked in Bengaluru are present in Gachibowli, and
2. what Ownly must adapt, prioritise or fix there.

**Analytical analogy (not an equivalence).** The Starbucks Australia case shows that a proposition that works in its home market can fail elsewhere. Local habits, the jobs customers need done, incumbent alternatives and price expectations can all differ. We use this case only as a lens. We do not claim Ownly is like Starbucks.

## 5. Evidence labels (mandatory on every claim in every deliverable)

| Label | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **FACT** | Independently verified: at least 2 independent sources, a primary document, or our own observed measurement | Audit-observed final payable at a timestamp |
| **COMPANY CLAIM** | Stated by Rapido/Ownly or its executives, including when repeated by media | "50,000 daily orders" |
| **MEDIA REPORT** | Journalism not traceable to independent data | "Incumbents offered better terms" |
| **CONSUMER-GENERATED** | Reviews, Reddit, LinkedIn or social posts | A user-posted bill comparison |
| **HYPOTHESIS** | A testable statement we have not yet tested | H3.1 |
| **INTERPRETATION** | The analyst's reading of evidence | "Respondents appear to anchor on the discount line" |
| **ASSUMPTION** | A premise we act on without evidence, which the team can edit | Scorecard anchor values |
| **UNKNOWN** | Evidence not available | Hyderabad order volume |

**Evidence hierarchy for weighing conflicts** (strongest first):

1. Observed behaviour (audit measurements, fake-door actions, the aware→tried gap)
2. Recalled recent behaviour (last-order reconstruction)
3. Stated trade-off choices (choice tasks, bill comparison, fee ladder)
4. Stated attitudes and intent
5. Interviews: depth and mechanism, **not prevalence**
6. Reviews and social posts: issue discovery and severity signals, **not population estimates**
7. Company claims and media

## 8. Methodology challenges (critical review of our own design)

These are real weaknesses. The design mitigates them but cannot remove them.

1. **Timeline is the biggest risk.** The v1 plan assumed a 14-day window from 2026-09-03, which ends 2026-09-17. This scope (2 surveys, ~370 valid responses, 16+ interviews, a multi-day audit, a fake door) realistically needs **4–5 weeks** of fieldwork plus analysis. See `O_execution_checklist_and_timeline.md` for a full plan and a cut-down plan. **The team must confirm the real submission date.**
2. **Non-probability samples.** Every sample is a convenience or quota sample. Confidence intervals describe precision *if* the sample were random. They do not prove the sample represents Gachibowli. We report "among our respondents" and test robustness (with and without IIIT-H, with and without weights).
3. **Brand-blind tests are contaminated in Hyderabad.** Ownly already markets there, so some "blind" respondents will recognise the proposition. Mitigation:
   - capture unaided awareness first;
   - randomise blind vs branded arms;
   - report H7 separately for the unaware subgroup.
4. **The fake door cannot test whether demand exists for Ownly.** We must not impersonate Ownly or imply a real service. It can test *which framing* drives more behaviour among people reached through our channels. Traffic will be small, so results are labelled **DIRECTIONAL** unless the pre-computed minimum sample is reached.
5. **Bengaluru survivorship bias.** Ownly users who are easiest to reach are active, satisfied users. We deliberately recruit lapsed users (tried, but no order in the last 4 weeks) and analyse them separately.
6. **Bengaluru and Hyderabad users are at different maturity.** Bengaluru users have months of tenure. Hyderabad users are in launch-offer weeks. A Hyderabad-vs-Bengaluru difference may be a tenure effect, not a city effect. We record tenure and compare early-tenure users where n allows.
7. **Stated choice ≠ market behaviour.** Choice tasks remove habit, app defaults and real offers. Implied rupee-per-minute values are **upper-bound preference signals**, calibrated against the audit and interviews.
8. **Offers are personalised.** Audit prices vary by account state and subscription. We record account state and never generalise one account's checkout to "the platform price".
9. **"Offline price" claims need proof.** We only compute premium vs offline where a dated menu photo or bill verifies the offline price.
10. **Reviews and LinkedIn are skewed.** App reviews skew to extremes. LinkedIn skews to PR and industry commentary. They find issues; they don't measure prevalence.
11. **Occupation is confounded.** Students and professionals differ in income, living arrangement and frequency. H6 tests use segment comparisons *and* models controlling for frequency and spend, so "student" is not confused with "low spender".
12. **Answering "what drove adoption in Bengaluru" causally is not possible** with our data. We can report drivers *as stated and correlated* among reached users.

## 9. Standing research rules (from the brief)

- No fabricated responses, quotes, reviews or statistics.
- Intent is not behaviour.
- Reviews are not population data.
- Bengaluru is not Hyderabad.
- Students are not professionals.
- Correlation is not causation.
- Statistical significance is not business significance.
- A low-price proposition is validated only when users trade something for it.
- Show contradictions.
- Keep work reproducible, and keep every number traceable to its source.
- AI use follows `context.md` §3a: AI never generates respondent data, and human review is required for coding and for the final decision.
