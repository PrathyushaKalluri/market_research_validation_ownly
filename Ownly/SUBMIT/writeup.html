Which parts of Ownly's Bengaluru playbook should survive Hyderabad?

A market research and validation study of price-led challenger adoption in the Gachibowli catchment, Hyderabad.

Compiled 21 September 2026 · Fieldwork September 2026
Survey n = 40 in catchment (124 responses received, 87 passing quality checks) · Price audit 4 matched baskets across a 10-restaurant frame, one Gachibowli address, Wednesday dinner slot, 16 September 2026
Public text 420 social items, 275 YouTube comments, 37 app-store reviews — all hand-coded · Interviews 1 documented transcript plus team-reported interviews (count not recorded)
Experiment Randomised fake-door landing test — designed, built, powered, not fielded

Summary. Ownly's price advantage in Gachibowli is real, structural and larger than the saving people say they need. It was cheapest in four of four matched baskets, with a median saving of ₹114.50, and its non-food charges are 5.2% of the bill against an incumbent median of 23.4%. Unlike every challenger in the Indian record, that saving comes from removing fees rather than funding discounts, so it does not depend on subsidy.

It nevertheless faces two constraints the price advantage cannot solve. First, assortment — not price, speed or reliability — is the binding constraint on adoption: holding ₹30 constant, 92.5% of respondents would accept a 15-minute delay and 72.5% a materially worse reliability record, but only 35.0% would give up their usual restaurants (McNemar exact, assortment versus speed, p = 1.5 × 10⁻⁶). Second, 82.5% of the catchment holds an incumbent membership, which erodes the observed advantage from covering 81.1% of the market's stated switching threshold to 56.1%, and to 27.3% once a routine incumbent coupon is applied — at which point Ownly is ₹28 more expensive.

The recommendation is therefore to keep the fee structure and price transparency, adapt the comparative price claim, the restaurant strategy and the Rapido channel, and deprioritise delivery-speed parity and deeper first-order discounting. A pre-registered prediction that Ownly's ~17-minute delivery deficit would block adoption was falsified by the data.

Section 1The problem, and why it is not the obvious one

Ownly is a food-delivery platform backed by the bike-taxi company Rapido. Its proposition is that it charges restaurants no commission and adds no platform, packaging or surge fees, so the customer's final bill is lower than on Swiggy or Zomato. It launched in Bengaluru and is now operating in Hyderabad, including at the Gachibowli address used throughout this study.

The obvious research question — will anyone try it? — is not worth asking. Discounts reliably produce trial. The Indian market has seen this repeatedly: Foodpanda, Uber Eats India and Amazon Food each bought substantial early volume and each subsequently exited or collapsed. Foodpanda fell from roughly 200,000 daily orders to approximately 5,000 once discounting stopped. No challenger in the Indian record has demonstrated post-subsidy retention.

The question that matters is therefore about the second order rather than the first:

How large and durable must Ownly's price advantage be to break Swiggy/Zomato lock-in in Gachibowli, and what restaurant coverage, discovery and delivery experience are required so that price-led trial becomes repeat usage?

Because Ownly is already operating locally, the decision this study serves is not whether to enter. It is a per-tactic judgement — keep, adapt or deprioritise — on each element of the approach that was used in Bengaluru.

2.1 The paired trade-off design

The central instrument inside the survey is a set of three forced choices administered to the same respondents. In each, the respondent chose between an order costing ₹260 and an otherwise comparable order costing ₹230. The saving was held constant at ₹30 throughout; only the attribute sacrificed to obtain it changed — delivery speed in one, reliability in the second, and availability of the respondent's usual restaurants in the third.

Holding the money constant and varying only the sacrifice means that any difference in acceptance between the three is attributable to the attribute rather than to price sensitivity. Because the same individuals answered all three, the appropriate significance test is a paired one.

Section 3Sample

124 responses were received. These were filtered in sequence to the study population: 78 answered the Hyderabad branch, 52 of those passed the 20–35 age screen, and 40 fell inside the Gachibowli catchment, of whom 34 were in Gachibowli proper. All 124 records remain in the dataset carrying exclusion flags; none was deleted.

The sample is a convenience sample and skews student: 32 of 40 are students and 7 are working full-time. It is not a probability sample, and results describe this sample rather than the population of Gachibowli. Two sample characteristics are themselves findings:

  • 82.5% hold a paid incumbent membership (33 of 40; 95% CI 68.0–91.3). This is the single largest barrier measured in the study, because a membership converts a structural list-price saving into no saving at all.
  • 57.5% use more than one delivery app (95% CI 42.2–71.5). A multi-homing market allows Ownly to become a secondary app before it becomes a primary one — a lower bar than switching, but it also means trial does not imply displacement.

Section 4Findings

4.1 Assortment is the binding constraint

The three paired choices separate sharply. Respondents will trade time and reliability for ₹30; they will not trade their restaurants.

Table 2 — Share accepting the ₹30 saving, by what is sacrificed (n = 40, paired)
SacrificeAccept95% CIReading
Wait 15 minutes longer92.5%80.1–97.4Not a constraint
Accept a late order 3 times in 10 rather than 172.5%57.2–83.9Not a constraint
Give up most usual restaurants35.0%22.1–50.5The constraint

McNemar's exact test, which considers only respondents who changed their answer between two scenarios, confirms the separation is not sampling noise. Comparing assortment with speed, 24 respondents accepted a delay but refused to lose their restaurants, while exactly one did the reverse (p = 1.5 × 10⁻⁶). Comparing assortment with reliability, the discordance is 19 against 4 (p = 0.0026). The third comparison — reliability against speed — is significant at p = 0.021 but is the weakest of the three and would not survive a strict multiple-comparison correction; the two assortment comparisons survive comfortably.

This result is independently consistent with the price audit, which found 88.9% catalogue overlap between Ownly and at least one incumbent, and with the public record, in which commentators name the same constraint directly.

4.2 The price advantage is real, structural, and largely invisible

Across four matched baskets priced on all three platforms at the same address within the same twenty minutes, Ownly was cheapest in all four on list price plus fees, with a median saving of ₹114.50 (30.5%). Examining a single basket line by line shows where the advantage comes from: for an identical item at Cream Stone, Ownly's menu price was ₹179.00 against Zomato's ₹189.00 — a difference of ₹10 — while the total difference at checkout was ₹71.19. The remaining ₹61 was fees Ownly did not charge.

This distinction matters more than the headline saving. A discount is funded and can be withdrawn; not charging a fee is structural and requires no subsidy to sustain. On zero-discount captures, non-food charges were 4.8% of an Ownly bill against 15.1% on Zomato and 27.4% on Swiggy. On this mechanism Ownly genuinely differs from the challengers that preceded it.

The advantage is nevertheless largely invisible to the people who matter. Respondents were asked, separately, how much they would need to save on every order before changing their main app; the median answer was ₹30. Comparing each respondent's stated threshold against the observed saving for each audited basket produces 132 person-by-basket pairs, and the share of those pairs the saving clears collapses as the comparison moves toward a realistic checkout.

At the third row Ownly is not merely less advantaged — it is more expensive. Expressed as win rates across the four baskets, Ownly wins 4 of 4 on list price, 3 of 4 against a membership, and 2 of 4 once a coupon lands. The strategic implication is that Ownly is marketing a comparison its competitors can falsify, at will and at no cost.

4.3 Reliability is accepted at the point of choice and fatal after repetition

The survey and the review record appear to contradict each other. In a single forced choice, 72.5% of respondents accepted a materially worse reliability record to save ₹30. Yet among app-store reviews, fulfilment failure is mentioned in 72.4% of the 29 coded reviews, and support or refund problems in 79.3%. The pattern repeats in social text: among posts from people describing their own Ownly order, 49.5% mention a failed order, against 18.9% in general discussion of the brand.

The interviews resolve this. Respondents report leaving an app after repeated bad orders rather than after one. Users therefore accept a reliability risk at the moment of choosing and act on it only after it recurs — two different decision moments, both true. The consequence for this study is specific: unreliability does not block trial; it blocks the habit, which is precisely the outcome the research question concerns.

These shares should not be read as failure rates. Public posts are self-selected, and dissatisfied customers post far more readily than satisfied ones. The informative quantity is the difference between first-hand and general discussion, not the level of either.

4.4 Rapido supplies access, not discovery

Ownly's most frequently claimed structural asset is its presence inside Rapido, an application users already have. The data does not support the claim that this converts. Of 40 respondents, 24 had used Rapido in the previous four weeks; of the 33 who answered the question about what they had noticed inside the app, only 5 (15.2%, 95% CI 6.7–30.9) had noticed food there at all. These two figures rest on different denominators and are not combined.

The resulting funnel is steeply attenuating: 42.5% had heard of Ownly, 17.5% had opened it, and 5.0% had ordered. The final figure represents 2 respondents of 40, with a confidence interval running from 1.4% to 16.5%; it is reported as directional only, and the four-week reference window largely predates Ownly's Hyderabad rollout, so a low value measures the question window as much as it measures rejection.

The interviews again redirect the interpretation. Respondents describe discovering new apps through friends, Instagram and YouTube rather than through in-app placement; the documented interviewee had heard of Ownly from her brother. The implied intervention is therefore a referral or creator-led trial mechanism, not a change to where a button sits.

4.5 The proposition itself did not separate

Respondents were shown two bundled propositions — a Bengaluru-style price-and-transparency pitch and a Hyderabad-localised pitch emphasising local restaurants, honest delivery times and fast redress — and asked to choose. Among the 29 expressing a preference, 62.1% chose the Bengaluru-style proposition, which an exact binomial test cannot distinguish from chance (p = 0.265, 95% CI 42.3–79.3). A paired McNemar test on top-two-box trial intent for the same two propositions returns p = 1.00.

Eleven of 40 respondents (28%) could not choose between the two at all. When more than a quarter of an audience cannot differentiate two positioning strategies, that is itself a finding about how distinct the strategies are — and it is the principal reason the fake-door experiment was designed.

At n = 40 only large effects are detectable, so a failure to reject should be read as "this sample cannot see a difference" rather than as evidence that none exists. The null results are retained because suppressing them would misrepresent the study, and because they identify precisely which questions require a larger sample.

One pre-registered prediction was falsified. Prediction P3 held that Ownly's approximately 17-minute delivery-time deficit would constitute a barrier to adoption. It does not: 92.5% of respondents accepted a 15-minute delay for ₹30. The prediction was recorded before data collection and is reported as overturned.

Section 6Metrics framework

Twenty-three KPIs were specified across inputs, outputs and a guardrail, together with ten marketing metrics. Fourteen KPIs and all ten marketing metrics were computed. Nine KPIs and ten further standard marketing metrics are marked NOT ESTIMABLE, with a stated reason, rather than imputed.

Three of the ten computed marketing metrics are definitions constructed for this study, because the standard formula set contains no metric for the question actually at issue — whether a saving is large enough for the specific people being asked. These are Switch-Threshold Coverage (81.1%), Offer Reversal Rate (50.0%) and Membership Erosion Rate (25.0%).

The metrics that could not be computed are, collectively, the ones that determine whether Ownly survives: retention rate, customer lifetime value, customer acquisition cost, contribution margin per order, market share and EBITDA. Every one requires Ownly's or Rapido's internal transaction and cost data. The study's own audit observed Ownly charging ₹0 in delivery, platform and packaging fees while taking no commission, meaning observed revenue per order at this address was ₹0 — an observation about the customer's bill, not a claim about the company's accounts. That these metrics are unavailable to any external researcher is itself a material finding about the evaluability of the business model.

Section 7Recommendations

Ownly has escaped the mechanism that destroyed Foodpanda and Uber Eats India: its saving is structural rather than rented. It has, however, inherited two others. It attacks the side of the market that was never scarce — restaurants already list on every platform — and it offers no new use case, since 88.9% of its catalogue is available elsewhere.

Section 8Limitations

  • Nothing here is behaviour. Every switching, trial and repeat figure is stated intent, observed price, or public text. No respondent was observed choosing.
  • The sample is small and not representative. n = 40, convenience-sampled, student-skewed. Confidence intervals are reported on every proportion precisely because point estimates would overstate what the sample supports.
  • The price audit is a snapshot. Four baskets, one address, one Wednesday dinner slot. It establishes that the gap existed at that moment, not that it holds generally. The Ownly account was new and the incumbent accounts were subscribed.
  • The audited restaurants were showcase chains. Ownly lists 221 restaurants at the audited address; ten were sampled, selected in a way that favours restaurants present on every platform and cannot detect exclusives. This directly limits the assortment finding.
  • Delivery time is quoted, not measured. No test orders were placed, so on-time performance and cancellation rates are unknown. The inference that the flat +17-minute gap reflects supply density is a hypothesis; outlet distances were not recorded.
  • Public text is self-selected. Coded shares describe the conversation, not incidence.
  • The interview count is unknown. One transcript is documented; others were conducted but not logged. No interview finding carries a prevalence figure, and the documented notes were machine-generated and not verified verbatim or double-coded.

Section 9Further research

  1. Field the fake-door experiment. It is the only instrument in the design that produces behavioural first-order conversion, which is what H₀ concerns. The landing page is built, the event collector is verified operational, and the design uses no company's name, logo or interface. At 200 visitors it would detect a difference of approximately 20 percentage points at 80% power; at 500, approximately 12.
  2. Audit named local restaurants rather than showcase chains. The recommended strategy depends on depth in exactly the segment the current audit frame cannot see.
  3. Place test orders. Quoted ETA is not delivery performance, and reliability is the identified churn driver.
  4. Document the interviews already conducted — establish the count, back-fill the tracker and verify quotes — before conducting further ones.
  5. Re-run the price audit from a membership-holding account, which is what 82.5% of the target population actually sees.
