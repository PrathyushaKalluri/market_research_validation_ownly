01 · Are we in the right direction?

Yes. Here is the test, and you pass it.

The project was set up to answer one question: which parts of Ownly's Bengaluru playbook should be kept, adapted or deprioritised for Hyderabad, backed by data. That is the deliverable. Everything else is supporting material.

Verdict

You can answer that question for seven separate tactics, and every single call is backed by a number you collected yourself — not by an opinion, and not by something you read online.

That is the whole assignment. It is done. The rest of this page is about presenting it and defending it, not about collecting more.

Why your evidence is strong enough

  • You have four independent kinds of evidence — a survey, a price audit you ran yourself, 732 hand-coded public posts, and interviews. When four different methods point the same way, that is called triangulation, and it is the thing that makes a small sample defensible.
  • Your central finding survives a real statistical test. The ₹30 result is not a difference in percentages you eyeballed — it is McNemar's exact test at p = 0.0000015, on a paired design where the same people answered all three questions.
  • You wrote a prediction down before collecting data, and your own data proved you wrong. You predicted delivery speed would block adoption. It doesn't — 92.5% will wait. Falsifying your own hypothesis is the single most credible thing a research project can do.
  • You listed what you could not measure instead of inventing it. Ten standard marketing metrics (CAC, CLV, retention, market share) are marked NOT ESTIMABLE with a reason. Most student projects fill those in with guesses. Not doing that is a strength.
02 · Is anything missing?

One thing. It does not stop you.

Every number in the study is what people say, what the apps charged, or what people wrote publicly. None of it is someone actually switching apps. The instrument that would measure that — the fake door — is built, its data collector is verified working, and it was never given traffic.

QuestionAnswer
Does this sink the project?No You were asked what to keep and what to drop. You can answer that from what you have.
Should you run it before submitting?No It needs roughly a week of traffic to detect anything meaningful. You do not have a week.
So what do you do with it?Present it Show it as a designed, powered, ethics-safe experiment with a power analysis — and say plainly why H₀ is still untested behaviourally.

Why presenting an unrun experiment is a strength, not an excuse

Saying "here is the exact experiment that would settle this, here is what it would cost, and here is the smallest effect it could detect" demonstrates that you understand the limits of your own evidence. That is a higher-level skill than running one more survey.

The opposite — claiming your survey measured behaviour when it measured intent — is the mistake that loses marks.
