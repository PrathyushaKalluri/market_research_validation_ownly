The problem

A cheap first order is easy to buy. A habit is not.

Every food-delivery challenger India has seen bought its first orders with discounts, then disappeared when the discounts stopped.

Foodpanda went from roughly 200,000 daily orders to about 5,000 when it turned the money off.

So our question was not "will people try Ownly".
It was: how big and how durable must the price advantage be for a cheap first order to become a repeat habit in Gachibowli — and what else has to be true?

The finding

We held ₹30 constant and changed only what you give up for it.

Every respondent saw the same two prices three times — ₹260 or ₹230. The saving never changed. Only the cost of the saving changed.

Source · data/tradeoff_summary.csv · n = 40, paired — the same 40 people answered all three. 95% CIs: 80.1–97.4 · 57.2–83.9 · 22.1–50.5.
Is that real, or just a small sample?

McNemar's exact test — the right test for the same people answering twice.

It ignores everyone who answered both the same way, and looks only at the people who switched. If two things mattered equally, the switches would split roughly evenly.

Where the advantage goes

Most people in this market never see the price Ownly wins on.

People said they need a median ₹30 saving, every time, to switch their main app. Ownly saves ₹114 — but 82.5% of them pay for Swiggy One or Zomato Gold.

Ownly markets a comparison its rivals can falsify for free, whenever they choose.

Source · data/switch_threshold_coverage.csv · 132 person × restaurant pairs (33 who named a rupee figure × 4 baskets). 4 said no amount would move them, 3 didn't know — held out, not coded as zero.
Does anyone believe it will last?

No. And the people who have actually used it are talking about something else.

87.5%agree a new app's low prices rise once it gets popular95% CI 73.9–94.5 · n = 40
25.0%would keep using it after a ₹100 intro offer ends95% CI 14.2–40.2 · n = 40
49.5%of posts by people who actually ordered mention a failed ordervs 18.9% in general chatter

This resolves an apparent contradiction. In a single choice, 72.5% accept a less reliable order to save ₹30 — reliability is cheap at the moment of choosing. But interviews say people leave an app after repeated bad orders.

Unreliability does not block trial. It blocks the habit — which is exactly what we were asked to predict.

Source · data/kpi_table.csv K18/K19 · data/review_theme_summary.csv (97 first-hand accounts coded of 123; 264 general items coded of 420). Caveat: people post about bad orders far more readily than good ones, so both shares overstate failure — the comparison between them is what carries information.
Nobody is finding it

Rapido gives Ownly access to millions of phones. Access is not discovery.

24 of 40 used Rapido in the last four weeks. Of the 33 who answered, only 5 had ever noticed food inside it — a ~85% discovery gap.

Source · data/kpi_table.csv K14/K15/K16/K9. The 60% and the 15.2% have different denominators (24 of 40; 5 of 33) — we have not merged them. The 4-week window mostly predates Ownly's Hyderabad rollout, so the 5% measures the window as much as rejection.

The answer

The Bengaluru playbook, tactic by tactic.

Bengaluru tacticCallThe number behind the call
Charge no platform, packaging or surge feeKeepFees are 5% of an Ownly bill vs 23% on rivals. Structural — it survives with no subsidy.
"The total you see is the total you pay"KeepCheapest in 4 of 4 baskets. Median saving ₹114.50, clearing the stated ₹30 bar.
Headline "cheaper than Swiggy & Zomato"Adapt82.5% hold a membership; after one coupon Ownly is ₹28 dearer. The claim is falsifiable.
Stock the same big chainsAdapt88.9% of its restaurants are already on Swiggy/Zomato. Same shops, +17 min slower.
Rely on Rapido placement for growthAdapt24 of 40 use Rapido; only 5 ever noticed food in it.
₹100 off your first orderDeprioritiseOnly 25% would stay once it ends. This is the rented demand that killed Foodpanda.
Spend to match delivery speedDeprioritise92.5% will wait 15 min longer for ₹30. Our own prediction that speed would block adoption was wrong.
If you remember one thing

Ownly's price advantage is real and durable — it comes from not charging fees, not from burning money.

But it is aimed at the one thing this market will not trade, and it is invisible to the 82.5% who already pay for a membership.

Fix the restaurant list before spending another rupee on the discount.

What we would test next

Everything so far is what people say. We built the test for what people do.

A fake door: a real landing page, two versions, visitors split by coin flip, every tap recorded. Built and the collector is verified working. Not fielded — we report it as a designed experiment rather than pretend we have its results.

What we would not claim

The honest limits.

Someone might read this as…What is actually true
"Ownly is 30% cheaper."In 4 baskets, one address, one dinner slot. Directional, not a market price index.
"82.5% of Hyderabad holds a membership."82.5% of our 40 catchment respondents, who skew student. The interval runs 68–91%.
"Only 5% have tried it."2 people of 40, in a window that mostly predates the Hyderabad rollout. Interval 1.4–16.5%. Nearly no information.
"Half of Ownly orders fail."Never claimed. 49.5% is the share of posts by first-hand users mentioning a failure.
"The Bengaluru pitch doesn't work."We could not detect a difference (p = 0.265, n = 29). Absence of evidence, not evidence of absence — which is why the fake door exists.

Five of our seven formal tests found nothing. We report them because hiding null results is how research goes wrong — and because they tell you exactly which questions need a bigger sample.
