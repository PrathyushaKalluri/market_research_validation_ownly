# Ownly Hyderabad Market Research & Validation — Review Log

**Course:** Market Research & Validation  
**Project:** Ownly (Rapido Food Delivery) — Bengaluru playbook vs Hyderabad market validation  
**Primary target:** 20–30-year-old students and working professionals in Gachibowli / nearby Hyderabad catchment  
**Time box:** 3 days  
**Team size:** 3

---

## 1. Project framing

Do **not** frame the project as:

> “Should Ownly enter Hyderabad?”

Ownly is already available / being integrated into Rapido, so the useful business problem is:

> **Can Ownly successfully transfer its Bengaluru growth playbook to 20–30-year-old food-delivery users in Gachibowli, and which parts of the playbook must be adapted for Hyderabad to drive trial and repeat usage?**

This creates a clear business decision:

- **Replicate** a Bengaluru tactic
- **Adapt** a Bengaluru tactic
- **Drop / deprioritize** a Bengaluru tactic

The project is therefore not just a survey about whether users “like Ownly”; it is a **market-entry / GTM transferability validation problem**.

---

## 2. Freeze what “Bengaluru strategy” means

The research contains Ownly evidence from different periods, so avoid mixing pilot-era and current-era models.

For this project, define the **Bengaluru Playbook (current / Jul–Aug 2026 framing)** as:

### Consumer proposition
- Lower final food bill
- Transparent pricing
- No / low platform, packaging and surge fees
- Everyday affordability instead of only coupon-led discounts
- Discovery through the existing Rapido ecosystem
- Promotional / gamified first-order incentives

### Supply proposition
- Restaurant-friendly economics
- Large restaurant onboarding
- Strong local / regional restaurant assortment
- Low / zero commission positioning

### Distribution
- Ownly integrated inside the main Rapido app
- Leverage Rapido’s existing user base
- Start dense geographically and expand outward

**Important:** Do not mix early pilot fee structures with current positioning in the same comparison without clearly marking the time period.

---

## 3. Primary business question

> **Will the Bengaluru Ownly growth playbook transfer effectively to Gachibowli’s 20–30-year-old food-delivery users, or does Hyderabad require a localized proposition and GTM approach?**

---

## 4. Primary null hypothesis

The broad statement “Will the Bengaluru strategy work in Hyderabad?” is a business question, not a precise statistical hypothesis.

Use:

### H₀
> **Among 20–30-year-old food-delivery users in Gachibowli, a Bengaluru-style Ownly proposition does not produce a significantly different first-order conversion rate compared with a Hyderabad-localized proposition.**

### H₁
> **The first-order conversion rates are significantly different.**

This enables a real decision between:

- replicate Bengaluru proposition,
- localize it,
- or investigate non-message constraints such as reliability / assortment.

---

## 5. Landing-page / fake-door experiment

### Variant A — Bengaluru-style proposition
**Same food. Lower total bill.**  
No hidden platform / packaging / surge fees.  
**CTA:** Check Ownly in Gachibowli

### Variant B — Hyderabad-localized proposition
The final copy must come from early interviews. An initial placeholder could be:

**Your Hyderabad favourites. Transparent bill. Reliable delivery.**  
**CTA:** Check Ownly in Gachibowli

Keep visual treatment, CTA placement, length and layout constant so that the proposition is the primary changed variable.

### Core metric

`Conversion Rate = CTA Clicks / Unique Visitors`

Compare Variant A and Variant B using a two-proportion test or Fisher’s exact test depending on sample size.

---

## 6. Diagnostic questions around the main hypothesis

Do not build a giant hypothesis tree. Use the main H₀ and diagnose *why* the result occurs.

1. **Acquisition:** Does a lower final bill / no hidden fees cause first trial?
2. **Retention:** After trial, do reliability, ETA and fulfillment matter more than savings?
3. **Distribution:** Are existing Rapido users more likely to discover / try Ownly?
4. **Segmentation:** Do students and working professionals respond differently?
5. **Promotion:** Does the ₹50 / ₹100 game create genuine adoption or only reward-driven trial?

A high-value secondary proposition to validate is:

> **Price may acquire the customer; reliability may retain the customer.**

---

## 7. How existing secondary data should be used

Existing evidence is useful primarily for **hypothesis generation and research instrument design**, not for claiming population-level incidence.

For example, coded reviews and social posts point toward recurring themes such as:

- savings / lower prices,
- non-delivery,
- cancellations,
- late delivery,
- support failures,
- payment / order issues,
- missing items,
- restaurant availability.

Do **not** report percentages from scraped / coded review samples as if they represent all Ownly users.

Instead frame it as:

> **Secondary qualitative evidence identified two competing themes requiring primary validation: price advantage may drive trial, while fulfillment reliability may constrain repeat usage.**

# 17. ₹50 / ₹100 game / reward

Treat this as a secondary acquisition test, not the entire project.

Measure:

**Reward → trial** versus **Reward → repeat**

Ask:
1. Would this reward make you place your first order?
2. If the next three orders had no reward, would you still use Ownly?

Interpretation:
- high trial + low repeat = promotional acquisition, not durable product pull.
