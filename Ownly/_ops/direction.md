# direction.md — current strategic direction

**Set 2026-09-16.**

## What we are solving
Whether the **Bengaluru proposition** or a **Hyderabad-localized proposition** better converts
20–30-year-olds in Gachibowli into a first order — and then, separately, whether either of them
survives contact with the second order. Concretely, we are producing a **KEEP / ADAPT / DROP** verdict
on each of the six Bengaluru tactics, each backed by a named piece of our own evidence.

## What we are explicitly NOT solving
- Whether Ownly should enter Hyderabad (it is already there).
- Ownly's unit economics, CAC, CLV, or real market share — we have no data and will not invent it.
- Rapido corporate strategy, rider batching, new verticals, the Metro-button relocation.
- Any nationally representative claim. Everything is *"among the 20–30-year-olds we reached in the
  Gachibowli catchment"*, and the slides say so.
- More secondary research. **We have 68 claims, 37 reviews and 524 social items and only 4 of those
  items mention Hyderabad.** The marginal value of another scraped row is close to zero. The
  differentiator now is first-party Hyderabad evidence.

## The decision we are trying to reach
> **SCALE / ADAPT / TARGET SELECTIVELY / RETHINK** for Ownly in Gachibowli — with a named positioning,
> a named priority segment, and named guardrails, each traceable to a specific observation.

## Why this research step exists right now
As of this morning the project had **an excellent research design and zero primary data.** Every
instrument was built on 14 September and nothing has been fielded since. The binding constraint is not
design quality — it is that no respondent has been spoken to and no price has been observed.

So this work block does exactly one thing: **convert built instruments into live data collection.**
Everything that does not put an instrument in front of a human today is deferred.

## The four evidence streams and what each one alone cannot do
| Stream | Answers | Cannot answer alone |
|---|---|---|
| **Price audit** (observed) | Is there a real price gap in Gachibowli after coupons? | Whether anyone cares about it |
| **Survey** (stated, n≈80) | Does the gap clear people's switching threshold? Price vs reliability vs assortment | What people actually do |
| **Fake door** (behavioural) | Which framing makes people act, randomised | Whether action becomes an order |
| **Interviews** (n=8) | *Why* — the mechanism behind every number above | Anything about magnitude |

**No single stream decides the recommendation.** The verdict comes from where they agree, and the
places they disagree are findings, not problems to be tidied away.

## The shape of the answer we expect to defend
Our own consolidated evidence already points one way *(see
`01_secondary_research/consolidated/consolidated_evidence_pack.md`)*: price dominates **discussion**
(35% of public posts are price-doubt), fulfilment dominates **experience** (47% of first-hand accounts,
72% of app reviews). That is a hypothesis, not a conclusion — **Case B** in the decision tree
(price acquires, reliability constrains repeat). We are collecting the data that could falsify it, and
if Service P wins cleanly on both trial *and* repeat intent, we report Case A and say we were wrong.

**We do not pre-decide the answer.** All four cases (A/B/C/D) stay live until the data is in.
