Analysis Tab — Speaking Script

Nine charts, in the order they appear. Blue box = what to say. Grey box = what to answer if asked.

OPENING THE TAB This is where all our own data comes together. Nine charts. Every one of them has a plain-English note underneath it on screen, so nobody has to guess what they are looking at.

The whole tab answers one thing: we know Ownly is cheaper — but is cheaper enough, and cheaper at what cost?

CHART 1

What people will give up to save ₹30

SAY THIS This is the single most important chart in our study.

We showed the same 40 people three choices. Every time, the saving was exactly ₹30. The only thing that changed was what you had to give up to get it.

Look at the orange bars. 92% would wait 15 minutes longer to save ₹30. 72% would accept a less reliable delivery. But only 35% would give up their usual restaurants — which means 65% would rather pay ₹30 more and keep them.

So ₹30 buys you time. ₹30 buys you some risk. ₹30 does not buy you someone's restaurants.

WHY THIS DESIGN IS STRONG

Because the money never changes, the difference between the bars can only come from the thing being given up — not from how price-sensitive someone is. And because all 40 people answered all three questions, we can compare them against each other directly instead of treating them as three separate surveys.

IF ASKED — IS THIS DIFFERENCE REAL? Yes. We ran a paired test comparing each person's answers against their own other answers. Assortment against speed came out at p = 0.000002. Twenty-four people gave up speed but refused to give up restaurants; only one person did the opposite. That is not a coin flip.

CHART 2

How much cheaper Ownly has to be before people switch

SAY THIS Here we asked the question directly: how much cheaper does an app have to be, on every order, before you would make it your main one?

The bottom axis is the size of the saving. The side axis is the share of people who would switch at that saving.

At ₹10, almost nobody moves. At ₹30, half the group moves. At ₹50, nearly three-quarters move. By ₹100 you have everyone who is willing to move at all.

Now put that next to what Ownly actually delivers: ₹114 at list price. The saving is already well past what people are asking for.

THE TWO LINES

The solid blue line is the 33 people who gave us a rupee figure. The dashed grey line is all 40, which includes 4 people who said no saving would ever move them and 3 who did not know. That is why the grey line sits lower — those 7 are counted but can never be won on price alone.

IF ASKED — WHY TWO LINES INSTEAD OF ONE NUMBER? Because the answer changes depending on who you count. On the blue line ₹30 wins half the group. On the grey line it takes ₹50. Both are true. Showing one and hiding the other would be picking the answer we liked.

CHART 3

What happens to Ownly's price advantage as offers are applied

SAY THIS This is the chart that complicates the good news.

We took four dishes. For each one we opened all three apps at the same moment, from the same Gachibowli address, and priced the identical dish.

At list price, Ownly was cheapest on all four, saving about ₹114.
For someone who pays for Swiggy One or Zomato Gold, Ownly won three of four — the membership alone took away part of the gap.
Once a coupon was applied, Ownly won only two of four, and on average came out ₹28 more expensive.

In one case a Swiggy coupon turned a ₹205 Ownly order into a ₹45 Swiggy order.

Remember: 82% of the people we surveyed already pay for one of those memberships. So most of them are not looking at the price where Ownly wins.
IF ASKED — FOUR DISHES IS SMALL It is, and we say so. But these are real observed prices, captured at the same moment on all three apps, not estimates. The direction is consistent across all four and it matches what people tell us in the survey. A bigger audit is the second item on our next-steps list.

CHART 4

Delivery time shown at checkout

SAY THIS Orange is Ownly. Blue is whichever of Swiggy or Zomato was faster.

Ownly is slower at every single restaurant — and by a similar amount each time, between 17 and 24 minutes.

That pattern is the interesting part. If the problem were riders or traffic, you would see a couple of slow orders and the rest fine. A flat gap at every restaurant usually means something structural — it means the food is being picked up from further away.

And we found a likely reason. Ownly lists Bawarchi, but not the Gachibowli branch of Bawarchi. So it delivers from a further outlet. If that is happening across the app, then the slow delivery and the missing restaurants are the same problem — and fixing the branch fixes both at once.

CHART 5

Which restaurants each app carries

SAY THIS We checked ten restaurants at a Gachibowli address on all three apps.

Ownly carries nine of the ten. It is missing Pizza Hut. And one restaurant — Aanimuthyalu Unlimited — is on Ownly and on neither of the other two.

Ownly lists 221 restaurants in Gachibowli in total, so there are very likely more it has to itself than the one we happened to find. We only checked ten.

What it is clearly missing is the well-known names — ITC, Taj, Domino's, KFC, Pizza Hut. So the gap is at the famous end, not the local end.
IF ASKED — WHY ONLY TEN RESTAURANTS? Those ten were chosen so we could price the same dish on all three apps at the same time, which is slow to do properly. The wider check across Ownly's full 221 is on our next-steps list — that is the only way to size how much Ownly has that the others do not.

CHART 6

How Ownly is winning its first orders

SAY THIS These three are running in the live apps right now.

A game. Ownly has a retro racing mini-game inside the app. Win it and you get ₹50 off. You can play three times, so a new customer can earn ₹150 of discount just by playing.

A referral. Around ₹100 when a friend places their first order. That matters because our interviews say people try a new food app because a friend told them to — so Ownly is buying exactly the channel people actually trust.

And the app change. In the Rapido app, Ownly has just taken Metro's place in the bottom bar. Metro has moved to a box on the home screen. Ownly now has the most valuable position in an app tens of millions of people already open.

CHART 7

Which groups behave differently

SAY THIS Same three trade-offs, now split by group. Two things stand out.

Working professionals want a bigger saving than students — ₹50 against ₹30. They earn more, so ₹30 moves them less.

And the one that matters most for strategy: people who do not pay for a membership are about twice as likely to keep using Ownly after an introductory offer ends — 43% against 21%.

That tells us who to go after first. Not everyone. The people who are not already locked into Swiggy One or Zomato Gold.

Notice also that restaurant choice is protected hardest in every single group. There is no segment where ₹30 buys someone's restaurants.

The table under the chart has the full numbers for every group, including how many people are in each one. The professional group is only 7 people, so we read it as a direction, not a measurement.

CHART 8

Gachibowli against Bengaluru, and where orders go today

SAY THIS Bengaluru is our benchmark. Ownly has been live there far longer.

In Bengaluru, 75% know Ownly and 25% have ordered. In Gachibowli it is 42% and 5%.

That gap is not bad news — it is what growth looks like. Bengaluru is roughly where Gachibowli could be in a few months. It tells us Hyderabad is early, not failing.

One thing is identical in both cities: around 80% of people expect a new app's low prices to go up once it becomes popular. That scepticism is not a Hyderabad problem, it is a category problem.

And the second chart shows what Ownly is up against. In Gachibowli, Swiggy takes 55% of orders and Zomato 37%. More than nine in ten orders go to those two. That is the habit the price advantage has to break.
IF ASKED — HOW MANY PEOPLE IN BENGALURU? Sixteen. It is a small benchmark and we treat it as context, never mixed in with the Hyderabad numbers. But it is also the only place in our study with people who have genuinely used Ownly — four of them — and their reasons for using it less are useful: two said the offers ended, one had late or failed deliveries, and one went back to their Zomato Gold membership.

CHART 9

What we tested for a relationship, and what we found

SAY THIS Before we looked at the data we wrote down four relationships we wanted to test. This chart shows what came back.

Each dot is how strong the relationship was. The line through it is the range the true answer is likely to sit in. The vertical line in the middle means no relationship at all.

All four ranges cross that middle line. So none of these four is strong enough to build a decision on — with 40 people you can only detect a very large effect, and these are not large.

We are showing it rather than hiding it, because the alternative is testing twenty things and reporting only the one that happened to look interesting. Our recommendations come from the trade-off and price findings instead, where the differences are big and clear.

Closing the tab

SAY THIS So pulling the whole tab together:

The price is right. People want ₹30 to ₹50. Ownly delivers ₹114.
But most people never see it. 82% have a membership, and a membership plus a coupon wipes the advantage out.
Speed is not the problem. 92% will happily wait longer to save ₹30.
Restaurants are the problem. It is the only thing money could not buy, in every single group.
And the delivery gap and the restaurant gap may be one problem — Ownly carrying the brand but not the nearest branch.

That is what leads directly into our recommendations.
