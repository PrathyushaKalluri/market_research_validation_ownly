01The problem

Which parts of the Bengaluru playbook should Gachibowli KEEP, ADAPT or DEPRIORITISE?

That is the decision this study serves. Getting to a question that sharp took three attempts.

Three versions

How the question sharpened

FIRST VERSION A market-research question

“Should Ownly enter Hyderabad?”

Ownly is already here — a restaurant-partner launch with the National Restaurant Association in Jubilee Hills on 22 July 2026, then on-ground promotion in September. The company has answered this one.

→
SECOND VERSION A transfer question

“Does the Bengaluru playbook transfer to Gachibowli?”

Closer, but nothing in it can be measured. “Transfer” has no number attached, so no answer to it tells anyone what to change.

→
THE BUSINESS QUESTION What we set out to answer

How large and durable must Ownly's price advantage be to break the Swiggy and Zomato habit in Gachibowli, and what restaurant choice, discovery and delivery experience are needed so that a cheap first order becomes a regular one?

Every part of this is measurable, and every part maps to a KPI on the next page.

The approach

What we set out to test

The idea being tested

A lower price gets someone to try Ownly. The restaurants it carries and the delivery it provides decide whether they come back.

Price, restaurant choice, discovery and delivery are measured separately, so each can be judged on its own evidence.

How we answered it

A survey of people who order food in the Gachibowli catchment, a price audit comparing the same dishes across all three apps at the same moment, interviews, app-store and social listening, and a study of every company that has tried this before in India.

Each source is reported separately, so it is always clear where a number came from.

How Ownly is winning its first orders

THE GAME₹50 off, up to three times

Ownly runs a retro racing mini-game inside the app. Win it and you get ₹50 off your order. It can be played a maximum of three times, so a new customer can earn ₹150 of discount through play alone.

THE REFERRAL₹100 for a friend's first order

Users can refer friends, and the referral pays out around ₹100 on that friend's first order. This is Ownly buying word of mouth directly — the exact channel our interviews say people trust most.

THE APP CHANGEOwnly took Metro's place in the bottom bar

In the Rapido app, Ownly now sits in the bottom navigation bar where Metro used to be, and Metro has moved to a box on the home screen. Ownly has been given the most valuable position in an app tens of millions of people already open.

All three are running right now. Together they show a company buying trial through play, through friends, and through the most valuable piece of screen space it owns.

06Why others failed

Six companies have tried to beat Swiggy and Zomato on price. Ownly is playing a different game.

Every one of them bought orders with discounts. Ownly removes fees instead — an advantage that stays when the marketing money stops.

Five ways challengers have failed, and where Ownly stands on each

M1Demand bought with discounts disappears when the discounts stopSTRUCTURALLY DIFFERENT
WHAT HAPPENED BEFORE

Foodpanda India went from around 200,000 orders a day to around 5,000 once it stopped discounting. Orders on the government-backed ONDC network fell about 29% after incentives were cut.

WHERE OWNLY STANDS

Ownly's saving comes from removing fees, not from funding discounts. Charges other than food are 5% of an Ownly bill against about 23% on Swiggy and Zomato. That advantage does not need marketing money behind it.

M2Winning restaurants is easier than winning customersMOVING FAST
WHAT HAPPENED BEFORE

Zomato told the competition regulator that only about 1% of its restaurants are exclusive to it. Thrive closed its consumer app even while charging restaurants just 3%.

WHERE OWNLY STANDS

Zero commission has brought Ownly to 221 restaurants in Gachibowli within weeks of launch, including at least one that neither Swiggy nor Zomato carries.

M3Being cheaper is not the same as being differentTHE REAL BATTLEGROUND
WHAT HAPPENED BEFORE

Eternal, Zomato's parent, told shareholders in July 2026 that new low-price apps offer “the same restaurants, similar or longer delivery times”.

WHERE OWNLY STANDS

Our survey says the part that matters is restaurant choice, not speed: 92% of people will wait longer to save ₹30, but only 35% will give up their usual restaurants. Ownly's opening is to carry the branches people already order from.

M4Cheap food still has to arriveTHE RETENTION GUARDRAIL
WHAT HAPPENED BEFORE

Ola withdrew from ONDC after complaints about tracking, refunds and support. Zomato's own customers kept ordering through the price war because the service held.

WHERE OWNLY STANDS

72% of people will accept a slightly less reliable delivery to save ₹30, so reliability is not what stops someone trying Ownly. Interviews are clear that repeated bad orders are what makes people leave — so this protects the second order, not the first.

M5The economics have to work eventuallyA DELIBERATE CHOICE
WHAT HAPPENED BEFORE

Uber Eats India lost about $2.55 on an average order worth about $2.45 — more per order than the order itself was worth — and sold to Zomato.

WHERE OWNLY STANDS

Ownly charges no delivery fee, no platform fee and no packaging fee, and takes no commission from restaurants. Rapido has said openly that it is not trying to make money from food yet; the riders and the app are already paid for by the bike-taxi business.

Drawn from a study of Uber Eats India, Foodpanda, Amazon Food, ONDC, Thrive and DotPe — five research dossiers and around 230 sources.

The strategic logic

Why Rapido entered anyway

  • A customer the big two do not serve. Swiggy and Zomato are built around a higher-spending customer. A much larger group spends around ₹100 a meal and is priced out of app delivery entirely.
  • Fees, not discounts. No commission for restaurants, and no platform, packaging or surge fee for customers. The price gap is built into the model.
  • A fleet that already exists. Rapido's riders and app are paid for by bike taxis, and mealtimes fall outside the commute rush.
  • Restaurants were ready to listen. Ownly launched into an open dispute over commissions, with a National Restaurant Association partnership in Hyderabad in July 2026.
07H₀ decision

Price leads. Restaurants decide.

We put the two stories head to head with the same people, then took them apart to see which part was doing the work.

Read this first

In plain words

WHAT WE ASKED

We wrote two descriptions of a food app and showed both to the same people.

Service P — the Bengaluru story: a lower final bill, no hidden fees, clear pricing.
Service Q — the Hyderabad story: local restaurants, delivery that turns up, real support, clear pricing.

Then: if only one of these existed where you live, which would you start using?

WHAT CAME BACK

18 chose the price story. 11 chose the restaurants-and-service story. 11 wanted both.

Among those who picked a side, that is 62% for price — a clear lean, in the direction the Bengaluru playbook assumes.

WHAT IT MEANS

Price leads, and it leads comfortably. With a group of this size that is a strong directional signal pointing the same way as every other price finding in this study.

The 11 people who wanted both are the more interesting result: a third of the group did not see these as alternatives at all.

WHAT COMES NEXT

The sharpest version of this question is a live test where people can click and order, so that real first orders are counted rather than stated preference.

That test is designed and ready to run. It is the first item on the plan.

The sharper question

What the same ₹30 can and cannot buy

Asking people to choose between two whole packages hides what is driving the choice.

Each description carried four features at once, and 11 people said they wanted both — which tells us the packages were not really alternatives.

So we asked it a sharper way: hold the saving at ₹30 and change only one thing at a time. That separated cleanly, and it is what the recommendations are built on.

The position

Where this leaves the question

What we can say now

Price is the strongest single pull, and it pulls in the direction the Bengaluru playbook assumes. The saving needed is modest — ₹30 moves half the group, ₹50 moves a clear majority, and Ownly already delivers ₹114 at list price.

Restaurant choice is the one thing money does not buy. That is where the ADAPT belongs.

What the next test adds

A live landing test measures real first orders rather than stated preference, and settles the direction with behaviour instead of opinion.

The design is complete and the measurement plan is written. It is the first item on the next-steps list.

08Propositions

Five moves, in the order we would make them.

Each one says what to do, why it follows from the evidence, and which numbers support it.

The plan

1Lead with the everyday bill, not a comparisonKEEP

Ownly's saving comes from removing fees, so it is there on every order without a coupon. Say it as the price of the meal itself — “your usual order, ₹114 less” — rather than as a comparison against another app.

WHY

A comparison can be disproved by a single coupon on the other side. The everyday bill cannot. Eight in ten people here already pay for a Swiggy or Zomato membership, so a comparison is exactly the claim they are most likely to test.

THE EVIDENCE

Cheapest on all 4 dishes at list price · ₹114 typical saving · Fees are 5% of an Ownly bill against 23% elsewhere

2Win the nearest branch of the restaurants people already useADAPT

Target the specific outlets closest to Gachibowli for the restaurants people order from most, rather than adding more restaurant names to the total.

WHY

Restaurant choice is the only thing people would not give up for ₹30 — 65% paid more to keep it. Ownly already carries the big names, but not always the nearest branch, which is also the most likely reason its delivery times run longer. Fixing the branch fixes both at once.

THE EVIDENCE

65% protect their restaurants · Delivery 17–24 min longer at every restaurant checked

3Buy word of mouth, because that is how people actually arriveKEEP

Keep referrals and in-app rewards as the main route to a first order, and measure first orders earned per referral.

WHY

Interviews are consistent: people try a new food app because a friend told them to. Ownly is already doing this — ₹100 for a friend's first order, and a ₹50 reward playable up to three times. This is the channel our own respondents say they trust.

THE EVIDENCE

₹100 per referral · ₹50 game reward × 3 · Friend recommendation named the top trial trigger after price

4Use the Rapido bottom bar as the discovery engine it now isKEEP

Ownly now occupies the bottom navigation bar in the Rapido app where Metro used to sit. Treat that position as the primary acquisition channel and measure taps through to first orders.

WHY

Rapido opens in front of tens of millions of people who are already paying for a ride. Two-thirds of our respondents used Rapido in the last four weeks. Owning the best position in that app is the cheapest distribution any food app in India has.

THE EVIDENCE

67% used Rapido in the last four weeks · Ownly placed in the bottom bar, September 2026

5Make the second order the measure of successADAPT

Track what share of people who arrive on an introductory offer order again without one, and treat that number as the scoreboard for Hyderabad.

WHY

A first order is bought; a second order is earned. People who do not already pay for a membership are about twice as likely to keep using Ownly after an offer ends, which makes them the group to win first.

THE EVIDENCE

Repeat intent roughly doubles among people without a membership · 25% overall would continue after the offer ends

The next five things

What we would measure next

  1. A live landing test. Two versions of the offer, real clicks, real first orders. This turns the price-versus-restaurants question from a stated preference into a measured one. Designed and ready.
  2. A branch-level price audit. Record the outlet name and distance next to each price, confirming whether the delivery gap is a branch question.
  3. A wider restaurant check. Sample 25–30 restaurants from Ownly's own 221 to size how much it carries that the other two do not.
  4. Three test orders. Compare the delivery time promised at checkout with the time the food actually arrives.
  5. One extra survey question. “Name the three places you order from most” turns restaurant coverage into the version that predicts behaviour.
