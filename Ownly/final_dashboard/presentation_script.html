Ownly in Gachibowli — Presentation Script

Market Research & Validation · What to say on each screen, in order

Blue boxes are the words to say. Everything else is background if you are asked.

Opening — 30 seconds

SAY THIS Rapido has launched a food delivery app called Ownly. It is already live in Gachibowli. It is cheaper than Swiggy and Zomato because it removes fees instead of giving discounts.

Our question is not whether Ownly should come to Hyderabad — it is already here. Our question is: which parts of what worked in Bengaluru should Gachibowli keep, which should it change, and which should it stop spending on?

We answered that with our own survey, our own price checks in the app, interviews, and a study of every company that has tried this before in India.

Screen 1 — The problem

SAY THIS We did not start with the right question. We started with “Should Ownly enter Hyderabad?” — but they had already entered, in July 2026, with a restaurant launch in Jubilee Hills.

Our second version was “Does the Bengaluru playbook transfer?” That sounds better but you cannot measure it. Nothing in that sentence has a number in it.

So we landed here: how much cheaper does Ownly have to be, and for how long, to break the Swiggy and Zomato habit — and what restaurants, discovery and delivery does it need so a cheap first order becomes a regular one? Every part of that we can measure.

Screen 2 — Who we studied

SAY THIS 124 people answered our survey. We narrowed that to the people this study is actually about: 78 in Hyderabad, 52 in our age range, and 40 who live or work in the Gachibowli catchment. Those 40 are the base for every survey number you will see.

Two facts about them matter most. 82% already pay for Swiggy One or Zomato Gold. And 58% use more than one delivery app in a month.

That membership number shapes everything, because a member sees a different price at checkout from everyone else.

Screen 3 — The KPIs

SAY THIS Eight KPIs decide whether this business works. We measured six of them ourselves. Two need Rapido's internal data, and we have listed those anyway so you can see the full picture.

The North Star — the one number that answers the question

D30 Retained Order Rate

What it is: of the people who try Ownly, how many order again within 30 days, and how often.

SAY THIS A cheap first order only matters if it becomes a second one. This single number tells you whether that is happening. DoorDash reports exactly this publicly. Rapido's own co-founder has said affordability is meant to drive repeat and frequency, not just volume.

We cannot calculate it because it needs Rapido's order history. So everything else we measured is designed to predict it.

The seven driver KPIs

1. Price Win Rate MEASURED

How often Ownly is actually the cheapest, on the same dish, at the same moment.

SAY THIS We priced the same dish from the same restaurant on all three apps at the same time — four dishes in total.
At list price, Ownly was cheapest on all four.
For someone with a Swiggy or Zomato membership, three of four.
Once a coupon was applied, two of four.

So the price advantage is real, but it shrinks depending on which price the customer actually sees.

2. Median Basket Saving — ₹114 MEASURED

The typical amount you save on Ownly at list price.

SAY THIS ₹114 on a typical order. That is a big number, and it matters because we also asked people how much cheaper an app needs to be before they would switch.

3. Fee Load — 5% of the bill MEASURED

How much of your bill is not food — delivery fee, platform fee, packaging, tax.

SAY THIS On Ownly only 5% of the bill is not food. On Swiggy and Zomato it is about 23%. This is where the saving comes from. It is not a discount — there is no marketing money behind it, so it does not disappear next month.

4. Required Saving to Switch — ₹30 MEASURED

How much cheaper Ownly must be, on every order, before someone changes their main app.

SAY THIS The middle answer was ₹30. Half the people who gave a number would switch at ₹30, and a clear majority at ₹50.

Ownly is delivering ₹114. So on paper the saving is far more than people are asking for.

5. Restaurant Coverage — 90% MEASURED

Whether Ownly carries the restaurants people want.

SAY THIS Of the ten restaurants we checked, Ownly carries nine. One of them — Aanimuthyalu Unlimited — is on Ownly and on neither of the other two. Ownly lists 221 restaurants in Gachibowli in total, so there are very likely more it has to itself than the ones we managed to check.

What it is missing is the famous names — ITC, Taj, Domino's, KFC, Pizza Hut.

6. Delivery Time Gap — 17 minutes MEASURED

SAY THIS Ownly quotes about 17 minutes longer than the faster of Swiggy or Zomato. The interesting part is that it is slower at every single restaurant, by roughly the same amount.

We found a likely reason. Ownly lists Bawarchi, but not the Gachibowli branch — so it delivers from further away. If that is true across the app, then the slow delivery and the missing restaurants are the same problem, and fixing the branch fixes both.

7. Rapido Discovery — 15% MEASURED

SAY THIS Two-thirds of our respondents used Rapido in the last four weeks. But only 15% of them had noticed you can order food inside it.

That is changing right now. Ownly has just taken Metro's place in the bottom bar of the Rapido app, and Metro has moved to a box on the home screen. Ownly now has the most valuable position in an app tens of millions of people already open.

8. Post-Promotion Repeat — 25% STATED INTENT

SAY THIS We asked: if you tried Ownly because of a ₹100 first-order offer, would you keep using it once that offer ended? One in four said yes.

And 87% agree that a new app's low prices usually go up once it becomes popular. People like the price. They do not yet believe it will last.

Also needed from Rapido

Contribution Margin per Repeat Order — what Ownly earns minus what an order costs to deliver. Ownly charges no delivery fee, no platform fee, no packaging fee, and takes no commission. Rapido has said openly it is not trying to make money from food yet.

That is the whole story of this project in one number. The saving is more than big enough. Whether the customer ever sees it is the real question.

Screen 5 — The analysis

The ₹30 question — our strongest finding

SAY THIS We showed the same 40 people three choices. Each time the saving was exactly ₹30. Only the thing being given up changed.

92% would wait 15 minutes longer to save ₹30.
72% would accept a less reliable delivery to save ₹30.
But only 35% would give up their usual restaurants.

So ₹30 buys time. ₹30 buys some risk. ₹30 does not buy your restaurants. That difference is far too big to be chance, and it is what our recommendations are built on.

How much cheaper is enough

SAY THIS ₹10 moves almost nobody. ₹30 moves half the group. ₹50 moves a clear majority. Ownly is at ₹114 — well past what people are asking for.

Where the advantage goes

SAY THIS At list price Ownly is cheapest on all four dishes, saving about ₹114. A membership on the other app removes part of that. A coupon removes the rest — and in one case Swiggy's coupon turned a ₹205 Ownly order into a ₹45 Swiggy order.

Eight in ten of our respondents have that membership. So most of them are not seeing the price that makes Ownly look good.

Who behaves differently

SAY THIS Working professionals want a bigger saving than students — ₹50 against ₹30.
And people who do not pay for a membership are about twice as likely to keep using Ownly after an offer ends. That is the group to win first.

Gachibowli against Bengaluru

SAY THIS In Bengaluru, where Ownly has been live much longer, 75% know it and 25% have ordered. In Gachibowli it is 42% and 5%. That gap is what growth looks like when it happens — and it shows Hyderabad is early, not failing.

Screen 6 — Why other companies failed

SAY THIS Six companies have tried to beat Swiggy and Zomato on price. All six struggled. We studied each one so we could ask an honest question: is Ownly doing the same thing?

The answer is no, in one important way. Every one of them bought orders with discounts. Ownly removes fees. When the marketing money stops, a discount disappears — a removed fee does not.

Screen 7 — The hypothesis result

SAY THIS We wrote two descriptions of a food app and showed both to the same people.

Service P was the Bengaluru story — lower bill, no hidden fees, clear pricing.
Service Q was the Hyderabad story — local restaurants, delivery that turns up, real support.

Then we asked: if only one existed where you live, which would you use?

18 chose price. 11 chose restaurants and service. 11 wanted both.

Among those who picked a side, 62% chose price. Price leads, in the direction the Bengaluru playbook assumes.

The eleven who wanted both are the interesting result — a third of the group did not see these as alternatives at all. That is why we went one step further and asked what the same ₹30 could buy, one thing at a time. That is where the answer came from.

Screen 8 — What we recommend

Hurdles and challenges we faced

1. Ownly is only weeks old in Hyderabad

It launched with restaurants in July 2026 and went live on the ground in September. Only 2 people in our sample had ordered on it. We cannot measure repeat behaviour for a product this new, so we measured the things that predict it instead.

2. We do not have Rapido's internal data

Customer acquisition cost, lifetime value, retention and profit per order all need company data. Rather than estimate them, we listed them as what Rapido should track, with the formula for each.

3. The survey form changed while it was running

Columns moved between exports, so our analysis had to be rebuilt to match questions by their wording rather than their position. It now survives further edits to the form.

4. Some answers contradicted each other

One person said they had ordered on Ownly and that their first order was in the last four weeks, while also entering zero orders for that same period. We report that as unavailable rather than guessing, and rather than calling it zero adoption.

5. The price audit is small

Four complete matched comparisons, one evening, one address. It is real observed data and it points clearly in one direction, but a bigger audit would make it stronger.

6. Comparing prices fairly is harder than it looks

The same dish has three different prices depending on whether you have a membership and whether a coupon is running. We had to report all three rather than pick one, which is why the price story has three steps instead of one number.

7. Age range

We intended 18–35. The form screened out anyone under 20, so what we actually collected is 20–35. We report it as collected.
